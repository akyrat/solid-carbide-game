## Drift marks

Task T-117 (Visual style, Decisions, "Drift marks"). While the car drifts, its two rear tyres leave marks on the ground. The scene is `scenes/driving/drift_marks.tscn` (script `scripts/driving/drift_marks.gd`); the run (`run.drift_marks`) and the drift prototype (`drift_prototype.drift_marks`) each add one and call `bind(car)`.

- **When:** only while the car's own drift state is on (`car.model.is_drifting`, the same one that gives likes for drifting: W, or S, held with A or D). Never while driving straight or standing still. Each drift starts a new mark per rear tyre (`marks_started` counts them), so separate drifts leave separate marks.
- **Where:** after every car physics step, at each rear tyre's real place on the ground, from the car's position, heading and size (Drifting, Decisions: 1 unit wide, 3 long): `rear_axle_units` behind the centre and `half_track_units` to each side (`rear_tyres(position, rotation)` returns `[left, right]` in units). A point is added once a tyre has moved `min_segment_units`. Points are in flat world pixels, so the node sits under the view's projection: in the run, inside the map's drawing (`map.view_root`), right after the ground tiles and before the buildings, arrows and boulders; the car (the run's `View`) and the HUD are drawn after the map. In the drift prototype it is right after the ground grid, before the test room and the car sprite, with the view's transform (flat or isometric). Marks stay where they were laid.
- **How long:** a mark is a chain of `Line2D` pieces. A piece closes after `piece_segments` segments, or when the drift ends; the next piece starts at its last point, so the mark stays joined. Each closed piece stays fully visible for `lifetime` seconds, then fades as a whole (the node's `modulate` alpha, never per pixel: Visual style, Decisions, "See-through rule") over `fade_time` seconds and is removed. Time runs in `_process` with the default (pausable) process mode, so marks do not fade while the game is paused. A new run builds a new map, which brings new, empty marks.
- **Cap:** at most `max_segments` segments, both tyres together; past it the oldest closed pieces go first (or, if only pieces still being laid are left, their oldest points). `segment_count()` is the count now.
- **Numbers (proposals for the board's playtest):** lifetime 6 s, fade 1 s, width 0.25 units (4 flat world pixels, about one tyre), cap 800 segments, pieces of 16 segments, a point every 0.1 units or more, rear axle 0.9 units behind the centre, tyres 0.375 units to each side. At drift speed a point is laid on every physics step (50 per second per tyre), so a continuous drift keeps about 7 s x 100 = 700 segments alive: the cap is a safety net above that.
- **Look (task T-118, a proposal for the board's review):** the scene's `mark_texture` is `art/effects/drift_mark.png`, an 8 x 8 tile drawn by a script (no PixelLab, board 2026-10-13): x runs along the mark, y across it, one tile is one period of the tread (0.25 units long at `width_units` 0.25, 32 texels per unit, the car and ground art's density). A chevron of 2-texel grooves (46, 43, 64) in deep rubber (12, 10, 20), between two ragged shoulder rows (27, 24, 40) whose gaps are the texture's transparent background. 3 colours, every one darker than the asphalt and the gravel, no semi-transparent pixels, not the heart's red. The rows inside the mark are fully opaque, so the mark never breaks up at a tile join. `texture_mode` 1 (tile), `mark_color` white (no tint), and lifetime, fade, width, cap and geometry unchanged from T-117. Each `Line2D` piece starts the tread at its own start, so where two pieces join, one chevron can be a little shorter; at this size it does not show as a seam. The record, with the tread and the palette, is `art/effects/drift_mark.json`; previews over both grounds and the unused alternative (transparent grooves) are in `art-archive/T-118/`.
- **Build and check the art:** `python game/tools/build_drift_mark.py build` (writes the PNG, its record and the archive previews; byte-identical every run) and `python game/tools/build_drift_mark.py check` (transparent background, no semi-transparent pixels, about 30 colours or fewer, no red, the rows inside the mark opaque, shoulder gaps of at most 2 texels across the join, every colour darker than both grounds; exit 1 on a fail). Python tests: `game/tools/test_build_drift_mark.py` (also fails if the committed PNG, record or previews are out of date). T-117's placeholder was a 4 x 4 one-colour texture stored in the scene itself, so there is no file to archive.

**Hype look (task T-249, a proposal for the board's playtest):** the marks heat up with the combo on the HUD's combo heat steps. `set_multiplier(m)` (the run connects it to `RunState.combo_changed`, and sets it at the start of each run) gives `heat_step = ComboHeat.step_for(m)`: 0 below x2 (the plain marks above, unchanged), 1 from x2, 2 from x3, 3 from x4; the thresholds are only in `ComboHeat.THRESHOLDS`. Each piece takes the step current when it is started and keeps it, so a long drift whose combo climbs shows the climb along its trail, and a mark laid hot stays hot after the combo drops. The drift prototype never calls `set_multiplier`, so it keeps the plain marks. The look of each step is in the scene's `heat_looks` (index 0 = step 1), all opaque, in the combo heat's yellows, oranges and white-hot, never red; the glow is a solid `Line2D` behind the rubber piece and the core a narrow `Line2D` on it, both fade with the piece's modulate:

## Drift smoke

Task T-121 (Visual style, Decisions, "Drift smoke"). While the car drifts, puffs of smoke come off its two rear tyres. The scene is `scenes/driving/drift_smoke.tscn` (script `scripts/driving/drift_smoke.gd`); the run (`run.drift_smoke`) and the drift prototype (`drift_prototype.drift_smoke`) each add one and call `bind(car, drift_marks)`.

- **When:** only while the car's own drift state is on (`car.model.is_drifting`, the same one the drift marks and the likes use). Never while driving straight or standing still (steering on the spot included). Each drift starts with one puff from each tyre at once, then `puffs_per_second` from each tyre (fractions carry over between physics steps). `puffs_made` counts every puff so far.
- **Where:** after every car physics step, at each rear tyre's place, taken from the drift marks' `rear_tyres()` (the node given to `bind()`), so smoke and marks start at the same spot; plus a random offset of up to `spawn_jitter_units`. A puff keeps its spot in flat world pixels and does not move with the car; it is drawn at `view_transform` x its spot, and drifts on screen by itself: up at `rise_speed` plus up to `spread_speed` outward in a random direction (drawn pixels per second). Puffs are drawn upright in screen pixels, not skewed by the isometric projection.
- **Life:** each puff lives `lifetime` seconds, varied by up to `lifetime_randomness` either way. Its size follows `size_curve` over its life (x 0 = born, 1 = end; y times `puff_scale`, the texture's scale): it grows, then shrinks, and pops out when its life ends. No puff fades: `puff_color` and the node's modulate keep alpha 1, and the texture has only opaque or fully clear pixels (Visual style, Decisions, "See-through rule"). Time runs in `_process` with the default (pausable) process mode, so puffs stand still on the pause screen.
- **Cap:** at most `max_puffs` puffs at once, both tyres together; a new puff past the cap replaces the oldest. All puffs are drawn by the one node (`_draw`), no node per puff. `puff_count()` is the count now, `puffs()` lists them (spot, drawn place, size, age, life, tyre).
- **Depth order:** in the run the node is a child of the run's `View`, before the car sprite: the smoke is drawn with the car, under the car sprite, and shares the car's place in the buildings' depth order (T-034, `View.z_index` from `map.car_z_index()`). So it is over the map's ground, drift marks and boulders (all at z 0 in the map, drawn before `View`, whose z is always 1 or more), under the HUD (a CanvasLayer), and a building that covers the car covers the smoke too. Puffs left far behind keep the car's current depth, so for their short life they can show over or under a building they are not next to. In the drift prototype it is after the ground, the marks and the test room and before the car sprite, and `set_view()` sets its `view_transform` (flat or isometric). A new run empties it (`clear()`) and binds it to the new car and map's marks.
- **Numbers (proposals for the board's playtest):** 50 puffs per second per tyre, lifetime 0.13 s +-25% (T-073 round 2, the board's playtest: the trail must span about 20 degrees of the circle in a full-circle drift; measured 20.2 degrees, and 138 degrees with the old 0.7 s and 30 per second; the arc is the live puffs of one tyre, oldest to newest, seen from the circle's centre, pinned by a test), cap 64 puffs, size curve 0.5 at birth, 1.3 at 40% of the life, 0.8 at the end (then pops; retuned in T-073), `puff_scale` 0.5 (the car art's density: the 24-texel puff is 12 drawn pixels at size 1, so a full-grown puff, 15.6 drawn pixels, is still about one car width, as with the placeholder), rise 10 and spread up to 8 drawn pixels per second, jitter 0.15 units. A steady drift keeps about 2 x 50 x 0.13 = 13 puffs, so the cap never trims. The whole puff does not fade as an object either (allowed by the See-through rule, but the pop-out keeps the pixel look crisp, as the level-up burst does); if the board wants a fade, that is a small code change.
- **Look (T-073):** the puff is one 24 x 24 sprite, `art/effects/drift_smoke_puff.png` (record `drift_smoke_puff.json`), drawn by `tools/build_drift_smoke.py` (no PixelLab): a lumpy cloud lit from the top left in five cool grey-violet tones (a faint cyan-tinted highlight, light, mid, dark, a deep violet rim), bands blended with a checkerboard dither and the outermost ring chequered away for a ragged, soft edge; no semi-transparent pixels, transparent background, no red. One sprite is the whole animation: the size curve grows it, then shrinks it. The root `texture_filter` stays Nearest. The old placeholder (a flat 16 x 16 round puff stored in the scene) is gone.

**Smoke sprite script:** `python game/tools/build_drift_smoke.py build` redraws the PNG, its record and the preview (`art-archive/T-073/drift_smoke_preview.png`, over asphalt and gravel at four sizes); `... check` runs the rules (transparent background, no semi-transparent pixels, about 30 colours or fewer, clear of the heart's red, cool colours, body at least 40 luma lighter than asphalt and gravel) on the committed PNG and exits 1 on a fail. Tests: `tools/test_build_drift_smoke.py` (Python tool tests).

**To swap the art or retune again:** change only `scenes/driving/drift_smoke.tscn`: `puff_texture` (opaque or fully clear pixels only), `puff_color` (a tint; white for the texture's own colours, alpha 1), `puff_scale`, `size_curve`, `lifetime`, `lifetime_randomness`, `puffs_per_second`, `max_puffs`, `rise_speed`, `spread_speed` and `spawn_jitter_units`. Keep the root's `texture_filter` Nearest. When and where puffs are made, and where the node sits in the drawing, are in the scripts and do not change. (`rear_axle_units` and `half_track_units` are only a fallback for when no drift marks are given; the run and the prototype use the marks' values.)

**Screenshots** of a run started from the main menu, the car drifting (smoke behind the rear tyres) and driving straight (none): `bash game/tools/screenshot.sh res://tests/fixtures/drift_smoke_demo_drift.tscn screenshots/drift_smoke_drifting.png 150` and `bash game/tools/screenshot.sh res://tests/fixtures/drift_smoke_demo_straight.tscn screenshots/drift_smoke_straight.png 90` (fixture `tests/fixtures/drift_smoke_demo.gd`). The car starts on the gravel; `drift_smoke_demo_asphalt.tscn` starts it on the asphalt road (`start_units` (54, 0)).

**Tests:** `tests/unit/test_drift_smoke.gd`, part of the GUT suite: smoke from both rear tyres on the marks' tyre places while drifting, none while driving straight or standing still, none after the drift ends; puffs staying where they were left while the car drives away, growing then shrinking and popping out within their lifetime, every puff gone once the longest life has passed, a 30-second drift never over the cap (and reaching and holding it with time stopped); the trail in a steady full-circle drift (W + D) spanning about 20 degrees (+-5); no aging while paused; the depth order in the run (in the car's drawing under the car sprite, over the map, under the HUD, under a building covering the car and over it in the open) and in the prototype (and the view switch); the puff art (nearest, no semi-transparent pixels, cool grey-violet, not the heart's red, no fading tint); the settings in the scene; a new run; a run started from the main menu.

## Boost effect

Task T-008. When W is newly pressed and that press snaps the car up to cruise speed, `Car` emits `boost(from_speed)` (forward speed just before the jump, units/s). It is `cruise_jump` filtered to `w_just_pressed`, so it does not fire while W stays held (not on the jumps back to cruise during a held drift) nor when W is pressed at or above cruise speed. It changes no driving. The art (T-009), the SFX Agent and the UI hook into this signal.

The effect is `scripts/driving/boost_effect.gd` (`run.boost_effect`, child "BoostEffect" of the run's View, under the car). T-008's placeholder streaks are replaced by T-009's PixelLab motion lines: `art/effects/boost_lines.png` (5 frames x 16 screen directions, 80 x 80 cells, drawn at scale 0.5 like the car) and its record `boost_lines.json`. Row 0 points right on screen, each next row is turned 22.5 degrees clockwise; the effect picks the row closest to the car's heading as seen in the view (`direction_row`), so the engine never rotates the art. Each W boost plays the five frames once at 14 fps (about 0.36 s, T-008's timing) and ends; a burst made through `bind(car)` follows the car, its lines' leading ends `behind_units` (0.8) units behind the car's centre, so they sit at the rear bumper (T-008's streaks stayed where they were made, and the car outran them at once). Clear of the heart's red.

**Rebuilding the sheet (free):** `python game/tools/build_boost_lines.py build 0,1,3,2,5` turns the archived PixelLab frames (`art-archive/T-009/call2/`, the frames used in that order; frame 4 and 6 to 8 came back empty) into the sheet and record; `check` runs the rules (no semi-transparent pixels, at most 30 colours, farther than 0.5 from the heart's red); `first-frame` redraws the first frame sent to PixelLab. Tests: `tests/unit/test_boost.gd` (T-008), `tests/unit/test_boost_art.gd` (T-009), `tools/test_build_boost_lines.py`. Screenshot fixture: `tests/fixtures/boost_effect_demo.tscn` (4 to 16 frames).

### Airtime trick (task T-242)

`car.gd` passes the step's `model.dt` as a third argument to `tricks.observe_air(airborne, turned, dt)`; the detector adds up the `dt` of the steps that end in the air. On the landing step, after the aerial tricks, it counts `counts["airtime"]`, sets `last_trick` to `"airtime"` (`TrickDetector.TRICK_AIRTIME`) and emits `airtime_trick(seconds)`, which the car forwards as its own `airtime_trick` signal. It is deliberately not in `TRICK_IDS` and not on the `trick` signal, because its amount depends on the seconds. The run connects it to `RunState.add_airtime_trick(seconds)`, which asks `ComboData.airtime_add(seconds)` (combo.json, `airtime`: `min_seconds` 0.3, `per_second` 0.2, `max_add` 0.6; seconds taken to hundredths, nothing under the minimum, result rounded to hundredths), raises the combo held at its cap, and emits `combo_trick("airtime", added, multiplier)`. The popup text is `TrickPopups.TEXTS["airtime"]` ("HANG TIME!", T-241). A caller that gives no `dt` gets no airtime. The drift prototype (no combo) shows no airtime popup; its detector still counts it. Proposals and reasons: Drifting, Content, "Airtime trick". Tests: `tests/unit/test_airtime_trick.gd`. Recording fixture: `tests/fixtures/airtime_gif_run.tscn` (`air_trick_gif_demo.gd` with no steering).


## Hype speed boost (task T-275)

The higher the combo's heat step, the faster the car (decision and proposed amounts: Drifting, Decisions and Content, "Hype speed boost"). `data/combo.json` `hype_speed` holds `bonus_per_step` (0.08, 0.16, 0.25 for steps 1 to 3) and `ease_per_second` (0.2); `ComboData.hype_speed_bonus(step)` and `hype_speed_ease_per_second()` read them. `RunState.hype_speed_target()` takes the amount for `ComboHeat.step_for(combo)` (the thresholds are only in `ComboHeat.THRESHOLDS`) and `eased_hype_speed(current, dt)` moves towards it. The run calls it once per car step (`Run._on_car_stepped`) and stores the result in `car.tuning.hype_speed_bonus`, and `CarTuning.top_speed()` is `forward_speed * top_speed_multiplier * (1 + hype_speed_bonus)`. The ramp and coast rates follow top speed, so acceleration rises by the same share; the time to top speed is untouched. The bonus is not a tuning-panel slider and the drift prototype never sets it (it stays 0), so the prototype is unchanged. A jump off a height-6 rooftop lasts 1.0 s, so it carries about 16.5 units at the base top speed and 20.6 at the top step. Tests: `tests/unit/test_hype_speed.gd`. GIF: `art-archive/T-275/screenshots/hype_speed_climb.gif` (a label shows the combo, top speed and speed). Fixture: `tests/fixtures/hype_speed_demo.tscn` (a drift circle, the combo climbing x1 to x4 every 2.5 s and dropping back); record it with Godot's movie maker and `tools/frames_to_gif.py`: `"$GODOT_BIN" --path game --fixed-fps 50 --write-movie C:/abs/m/f.png res://tests/fixtures/hype_speed_demo.tscn --quit-after 700`, then `python game/tools/frames_to_gif.py C:/abs/m art-archive/T-275/screenshots/hype_speed_climb.gif --skip 5 --fps 10 --scale 0.6 --shared --colors 64`.
