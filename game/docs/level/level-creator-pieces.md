  - **The road brush (T-263):** (Level design, Content, "Level creator: the road brush"; the points marked there are proposals). The Roads group's families are brushes (`"brush"` in a family of `creator_assets.json`: `"paint"` for Road, Centre line and Cracked road, `"zebra"` for Zebra crossing; `build_creator_assets.py` `ROAD_BRUSHES`). A brush family's look is an **asphalt** (the member's `"asphalt"`; its asset, the asphalt's straight tile along y, is only the button's and the strip's picture): Road and Centre line `a0` (M: `a0m`), `g6`, `g9`; Cracked road `g4`, `g5`, `g7`. R does nothing (the brush turns the road itself).
    - **Controls:** with a paint brush picked, press the **left button** on the map and drag: each 2 x 2 tile **block** (4 x 4 units, today's two-tile-wide road; blocks lie on the even cells) the mouse passes over becomes road of the picked kind and asphalt, with the blocks in between filled in so a diagonal drag leaves no gap (`RoadBrush.blocks_between`: steps along x and y). A **right-drag** erases road along it, and **Del** erases the block under the mouse. With Zebra crossing picked, a **left click** on a straight road lays a crossing across it on the row of tiles clicked. A blue outline shows the block (or the crossing's tiles) under the mouse, red while erasing. The top bar shows `road_brush_hint` ("Drag to paint a road", `creator_texts.json`). Esc ends a stroke and drops the pick. Editor functions (also for tests and fixtures): `brush_mode`, `brush_asphalt`, `begin_road_stroke(units, erase)`, `continue_road_stroke`, `end_road_stroke`, `paint_road(points)`, `erase_road(points)`, `place_zebra(units)`.
    - **How it picks the tiles (`scripts/level_creator/road_brush.gd`, `RoadBrush`):** a block is a road when its four cells are road tiles; its **arms** are the sides with a road block next to it (`arms_of`), which give its piece (`piece_of`): `straight_y` (n and s), `straight_x` (e and w), a T-252 corner, T-junction or crossroads, a dead end (one arm), or `single` (none: plain asphalt). `tiles_for(kind, asphalt, arms, zebra)` gives the four tiles: Centre line: `y_line_r` / `y_line_l` across a road along y (`x_line_r` above `x_line_l` along x), T-252's `piece_<piece>_<column><row>` on `a0` and T-263's `brush_line_<piece>_<asphalt>_<column><row>` on `a0m`, `g6`, `g9` (another asphalt falls back to `a0`); Road and Cracked road: `y_plain` / `x_plain`, a junction all `y_plain`, a dead end `brush_plain_dead_<side>_<asphalt>_<column><row>` (the kerb, no line). A crossing is the full `y_zebra` / `x_zebra` tile on both tiles across the road, on one row (along y) or column (along x) of the block. `paint`, `erase` and `zebra_at` change the blocks and then write the tiles of every block they touched and the blocks next to them in one go (`LevelDocument.set_cells`). What a block is (`state_of`: `kind`, `asphalt`, `zebra`) is read back from its tiles (`tile_info`), so painting next to a road saved earlier joins it when that road's tiles fill whole blocks. Painting over a road changes its kind and asphalt and keeps its crossing; a crossing goes when its block is no longer a straight along it. `blocked`: a block overlapping a building, platform or ramp is never painted.
    - **The new tiles** (no PixelLab; `python game/tools/build_ground_tiles.py`, then `python game/tools/build_creator_assets.py`): `build_ground_tiles.py` `brush_tiles` appends 268 tiles to the ground atlas after T-252's (indexes 139 to 406): the 13 T-252 pieces on `a0m`, `g6` and `g9` (`BRUSH_LINE_ASPHALTS`, `brush_line_*`, the same straight tiles' line pixels on each open side, so the line joins without a gap) and the four dead ends without a line on all seven asphalts (`brush_plain_dead_*`, the same kerb and gravel as T-252's). They are `"piece": true` in `ground_atlas.json` (catalogue `"part": true`, never panel looks). Every earlier tile keeps its index, so the game's own ground is unchanged.
    - **Saved levels** are the same ground data as before (a catalogue road tile per cell, `ground_rows` R under it), so a painted level passes `check_level.py` and plays, and earlier levels open unchanged. `LevelDocument.place` still places any single road tile or piece, as before.

  - **Closed ramps, arrows up or down, platform sizes (T-264):** (Level design, Content, "Level creator: closed ramps, arrows and platform sizes"; the numbers are proposals).
    - **Closed ramps:** `elevation_platform_drawing.gd` `textured_faces()` draws a wall at every ramp's tall end (its `join_edge()`) from the top down to what the ramp stands on, in the ramp side's texture, in every direction and height step. The east and south ends face the view; the west and north ends are drawn first, behind the top. The outline follows the wall. The game's own map and pieces that name an art piece are unchanged.
    - **Arrows up or down:** a ramp of any size has an optional `arrows` in the map file (`"up"` the slope, the default and what a ramp without the key means, or `"down"`). `GroundHeight` reads it, `arrow_direction()` gives the flat direction the arrows point, and the top face's texture runs the other way for "down" (the sign of v in `textured_faces()`). **A** turns the arrows of the next ramp (top bar buttons "Arrows up" and "Arrows down" show with a ramp picked); with a ramp selected and no ramp picked, A turns that ramp's. `LevelDocument.place(..., arrows)` and `set_ramp_arrows` write it. The preview shows the choice.
    - **Platform sizes:** with a platform (height 3 or 6) picked, the top bar shows S, M, L and "Any size (drag)" (`size_buttons`, `pick_platform_size`). A click without a drag places `PLATFORM_SIZES` in `editor.gd`: S 8 x 8, M 16 x 16, L 32 x 32 units; the default is M (it was the elevation sample's 84 x 30). "Any size" is dragging the size out, as before, and a click then gives M. A drag always sizes freely. Snapping and the overlap refusal are unchanged.

  - **Thread the needle (T-272):** (Level design, Content, "Level creator: thread the needle"; the choices and numbers are proposals). Pair two obstacles in the creator; in a run, driving between them is a trick.
    - **Obstacles and refs (one place, `scripts/level/obstacle_pairs.gd`, `ObstaclePairs`):** an obstacle is a boulder or a prop of the catalogue's `obstacles` group (`obstacle_props()`: `prop-barrier`, `prop-cones`, `prop-crates`). A level file's `pairs` is `[{id: "pair-N", a, b}]`, each ref a boulder's pattern copy id (`"boulder-3"`, a copy of one boulder: the creator's) or `"<copy id>/<index>"` (a copy of several, the game's own map), or a prop's id (`"prop-2"`). `resolve(layout, ref)` gives the obstacle and its collision (circle or rect, units), `line_of(layout, pair)` the line between the two centres (`a`, `b`) and its gap part from edge to edge (`gap_a`, `gap_b`), `crosses(from, to, a, b)` the crossing test. The run and the creator both use it; `check_level.py` (`check_pairs`) checks the same rules: two different existing obstacles per pair, no obstacle in two pairs, a copy of several boulders named with its index.
    - **Data (`LevelDocument`):** `is_obstacle(piece)`, `obstacle_ref(piece)`, `piece_of_ref(ref)`, `pairs()`, `pair_of(piece)`, `make_pair(a, b)` (a pair either was in goes first: the new pair wins), `unpair(piece)`. `delete` removes the pairs of a deleted obstacle and writes the other pairs' refs again (a copy's boulders shift when one goes). Saved with the level (`file_data`) and reopened.
    - **Controls:** **P** (`editor.gd` `KEY_ACTIONS`, `pair_pick`) or the top bar's **Pair obstacles** (`pair_tool`; `pair_button`, `set_pair_tool(on)`) turns the pair tool on (`pair_mode`; it drops the pick and the selection; the top bar shows `pair_hint`). A left click (`pair_click(units)`) holds the first obstacle (`pair_first`), the second makes the pair and shows `pair_made`; a click on anything else shows `pair_not_obstacle` and keeps the first; the same obstacle again lets it go. The tool stays on until P, Esc, a right-click or picking a piece. **Unpair** (`unpair_label`; `unpair_button`, `unpair_selected()`) shows with a paired obstacle selected. The bottom line is `controls_hint_pairs`. The overlay (`_Overlay._draw_pairs`) draws each pair as a dashed line across the gap and a ring round each obstacle in `PAIR_COLOR` (mint green), lifted to the height they stand on, and the held first obstacle ringed thicker.
    - **In a run (`scripts/level/thread_the_needle.gd`, `ThreadTheNeedle`):** `run.gd` makes one per map (`run.needle`, from `map.layout` and `ComboData.needle_rules()`), calls `observe(car centre, airborne, dt)` after every physics step and `touched(collider, shape_index)` on every `hit_obstacle` (a prop body's `prop_id`, a boulder body's `copy_id` and shape index name the obstacle). A pass is the car's centre crossing a pair's centre line (only its gap part can be reached); it fires `threaded(pair_id)` `clear_seconds` later unless either obstacle was touched from `touch_window_seconds` before the crossing until then; a pair pays again only `repeat_seconds` after it last paid; with `counts_in_air` false a crossing in the air does not count. The run then calls `RunState.add_needle_trick()`: the combo goes up by `trick_add.thread_the_needle` (held at the cap), the drain waits start again, and `combo_trick` is sent with the id `thread_the_needle` (`RunState.TRICK_THREAD_THE_NEEDLE`), so the popup shows **THREAD THE NEEDLE!** and "+{amount}" (`trick_popups.gd` `TEXTS`, normal size). A crash cuts the combo as before and pays nothing. **The marker (a proposal):** `build_markers(map, height_at, depth_for)` draws a faint dashed chalk line (`MARKER_COLOR`, alpha 0.35, 0.25 units wide) across each gap in `map.view_root` right over the ground tiles (`NeedleMarkers`), lifted and depth-sorted on raised ground as the drift marks are. The game's own map has no `pairs`: no gates, no marker.
    - **Numbers (`data/combo.json`, proposals with their reasons there):** `trick_add.thread_the_needle` 0.5; `thread_the_needle`: `repeat_seconds` 3.0, `counts_in_air` true, `touch_window_seconds` 0.5, `clear_seconds` 0.25 (`ComboData.needle_rules()`).

    - **One data file:** `data/level/props.json`: per prop `footprint_units` (facing north; east and west swap them), `height_units`, `collision` (`rect` = the footprint, or `circle` with a `radius`, in the middle of it), `label` (a `creator_texts.json` key), `look` (the parts the placeholder script draws) and `textures` (per rotation the PNG and `anchor_px`, the pixel standing on the footprint's centre on the ground; the art is drawn at `draw_scale` 0.5, 32 art px per unit). The catalogue's `prop` entries (`build_creator_assets.py`, `prop_entries`) are built from it. **T-256 swaps the art** by changing the PNGs in `art/props/` and the `textures` entries (the look and the footprint stay).
    - **Placeholder art:** `python game/tools/build_prop_art.py build` draws each prop's boxes and cylinders in code (no PixelLab; a top and two visible faces, a dark outline, a pale arrow on the top showing the facing, at most 30 colours, alpha 0 or 255) into `art/props/<prop>_<rotation>.png` and writes the `textures` key of `props.json`; `check` runs the rules (also no old-brand red).
    - **In the game (`scripts/level/level_props.gd`, `LevelProps`):** `mvp_map.gd` calls `LevelProps.build(map)` after its boulders: a `StaticBody2D` on collision layer 1 per prop (the car's `hit_obstacle` fires, which cuts the combo as for a boulder; minions and the kaiju are stopped), and a sprite in `map.prop_sprites` under `map.prop_sprites_root` (screen pixels, hidden in the flat view), lifted by the ground height under its centre. The sprites take part in the depth order like the boulders' (`map.depth_sprites()`: the car's, the enemies' and the elevation layer's covering tests and `restack`). `enemy_navigation.gd` closes the cells a prop takes (a rectangle like a building, a circle like a boulder), and `is_open_spot` and both enemies' `is_free_spot` refuse a spot inside one (`map.prop_blocks_spot`). The physics is flat: a car in the air above a rooftop prop crashes into it. The game's own map has no props.
    - **The level check:** `check_level.py` (`check_props`) checks each prop names a known prop and rotation, has that prop's footprint turned to that rotation, and lies inside the map.
    - **Final art (T-256):** the props use PixelLab art, `art/props/<name>_<rotation>_art.png` (8 generations, raws in `art-archive/T-256/raw/`), built by `python game/tools/build_prop_final_art.py build` (crop, at most 30 colours, red to orange, mirror for east and west; sets `props.json` textures and anchors) and checked by `... check`; test `tools/test_build_prop_final_art.py`. The placeholders (`<name>_<rotation>.png`, `build_prop_art.py`) stay on disk, and `build_prop_art.py build` redraws them but leaves the textures on the final art.   - **Road pieces (T-252):** corners, T-junctions, a crossroads and dead ends at today's two-tile-wide roads' size: each piece is 2 x 2 tiles (4 x 4 units), so each open side is a road's full width and joins the existing straight tiles (`y_line_r` / `y_line_l` along y, `x_line_r` / `x_line_l` along x, and the plain ones). `build_ground_tiles.py` appends 52 tiles to the ground atlas (indexes 87 to 138, named `piece_<piece>_<column><row>`, marked `"piece": true` in `ground_atlas.json`), so every older tile keeps its index and the game's own ground (`mvp_ground.json` cells) is unchanged (a test hashes it).
