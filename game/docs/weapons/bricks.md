## Bricks

Task T-324 (Weapon Behavior Agent): Bricks as Weapons, Decisions ("Bricks") describes it, with T-323's base numbers and, since T-496, the board's own tiers 2 to 7, and tier 8 (Widest Wave, T-498) (`data/weapons.json` "bricks" through `WeaponData.bricks_at(tier)`) and T-322's card texts (`scripts/ui/level_up_texts.gd`). What it does: `docs/weapons.md`, Content, "Bricks". The wave and the card image are T-325's art ([../art/bricks-art.md](../art/bricks-art.md)); T-324's code-drawn wave stays as the fallback for a radius with no sheet. No sound yet.

**See it:** set `bricks_at_start` to `true` in `data/playtest.json` (or take Bricks from a gift pack) and start the game (F5), then jump off a ramp or a rooftop's edge. Recording fixture: `tests/fixtures/weapons/bricks_landing_gif.tscn` (script `bricks_landing_gif_demo.gd`): a run from the main menu on the elevation map, Bricks at tier 1 and the gun off, the car driving north up ramp-nw-0-3 and jumping onto roof-nw-3; a crowd of 12 minions (6 tough) is placed round the landing spot as it takes off, out of its way in. Record with `"$GODOT_BIN" --path game --fixed-fps 50 --write-movie C:/abs/dir/f.png res://tests/fixtures/weapons/bricks_landing_gif.tscn`, then `python game/tools/make_turn_gif.py C:/abs/dir out.gif --glob "f*.png" --every 2 --size 680 460 --up 20 --scale 1 --ms 40`. The card: `bash game/tools/screenshot.sh res://tests/fixtures/level_up_screen_bricks_demo.tscn screenshots/bricks_card.png 90`.

**How it plugs in:** `scripts/weapons/bricks.gd` extends `weapon.gd` (`weapon_id` `bricks`, `starts_owned` false) and is listed in `weapons.gd`'s `WEAPON_SCRIPTS`; `run.weapons.bricks` (or `get_weapon("bricks")`). A new run resets it to tier 0 and forgets the airtime; `run.weapons.upgrade("bricks")` gives it and applies its tiers. `stats` is `bricks_at(tier)` with `damage` times `rack.damage_multiplier()`. Offered at a level-up as `get_bricks` / `upgrade_bricks` (in `level_up_offers.gd`'s `WEAPON_ORDER` and `ALL_IDS`), only while `weapons.json`'s `bricks.offered_in_level_ups` is true (`WeaponData.bricks_offered()`; T-324 set it true). **Playtest switch:** `data/playtest.json`, `"bricks_at_start"` (false); tests override it with the script's static `playtest_override`.

**The landing:** `step(dt)` reads the car's `airborne` (`car.gd`, from `car_height.gd`) and adds `dt` to `air_seconds` while it is true; the first step it reads false after true is the landing, `on_landing(seconds)`. It sends the wave when `seconds >= min_air_seconds` and `since_wave >= cooldown` (both from the data). It does not use the car's `landed` signal or the impact speed: the wave is the same whatever the height, and counting in the weapon's own step keeps it stopped with the rack (pause, end of run).

**The wave:** `send_wave(centre)` at the car's centre: every enemy of `targets(centre)` (since T-326 the enemies on the level of the ground at `centre`, `rack.enemies_at_level(rack.level_at(centre))`; [shared.md](shared.md), "Levels") for which `reaches(centre, e)` (its centre's distance minus its body, `rack.radius_of()`, is at most `radius()`) gets `take_damage(stats.damage)` once; then, when `pushes(e)` (the wiper's rule: an `apply_knockback()` call and not in the group `kaiju`) and it is still alive, `e.apply_knockback(away, knockback_distance)` with `away` from the centre to the enemy. Signals `enemy_hit(enemy, amount, pushed)` and `wave_sent(centre, hit_count)`; counters `waves` and `hits`.

**The drawing:** `BricksWave` (inner class), a projectile of the rack in its draw root (`rack.add_projectile`), so it stops while paused, is freed when done and is removed at a new run. It is placed at the landing spot through the isometric transform, lifted by the car's `height` (the roof's height on a rooftop), with the depth order of `rack.depth.z_index_for()` at that height. In stepped frames (`FRAME_SECONDS` 0.05) it draws the ring as the ellipse of a ground circle growing from `START_RADIUS` (0.6) to the damage radius over `RING_SECONDS` (0.35), easing out, with an inner ring behind it while it travels, then thinning until `WAVE_SECONDS` (0.6); plus 12 dust puffs and 8 brick chips (a seeded random spread per wave) thrown out and up, growing and then shrinking. Colours: `OUTLINE`, `RING_OUTER`, `RING_INNER`, `RING_CORE`, `DUST_DARK`, `DUST_LIGHT`, `CHIP`, `CHIP_DARK` (City colours, opaque, none of Greenbull's). The card's placeholder image is the `bricks` grid in `level_up_screen.gd`'s `PLACEHOLDER_GRIDS` (colours `r` and `n`), also the HUD's weapon icon.

**Tests:** `tests/unit/weapons/test_bricks.gd`: not owned in a new run and given by `upgrade()`; every tier is the data's (the board's tree, T-496; tiers 1 to 8 since T-498); a landing hits every enemy within the radius once (the kaiju stand-in by its body) and none outside, and nothing more on the next steps; the reach counts the enemy's body; a staged short hop and a real low drop under the car's gravity send none, a real jump sends one; a short and a tall real drop send the same wave (the same enemies, damage, pushes and ring); minions pushed straight away, the kaiju never, a killed minion not pushed; the cooldown; the garage bonus; nothing after the run; a new run; the playtest switch; the car's drive the same with and without Bricks; the ring growing to the radius and freed when done, wider at tier 7 (radius 8.4; the sheets are 4.0, 5.6, 8.4 and tier 8's 25.2), at the ground's height, and in opaque colours none of Greenbull's. Level-ups: `tests/unit/weapons/test_level_up_offers.gd` (taking `get_bricks` and every `upgrade_bricks` to tier 8 against `bricks_at()`), `tests/unit/test_level_up_screen.gd` (T-322's texts word for word on the cards), `tests/unit/test_weapon_data.gd`.

## Tier 8: the widest wave (T-498)

Weapons, Decisions, "The board's own upgrade trees" (Bricks tier 8, Widest Wave); what it does: `docs/weapons.md`, Content, "Bricks".

**Data:** tier 8 is `{ "effect": "widest_wave", "value": 3 }` and `bricks.max_tier` is 8. `WeaponData.bricks_at()` multiplies the radius by the value and sets `rings` to it (1 for every other tier); `ring_interval` (0.15, a proposal) comes from the bricks block. So tier 8 is radius 25.2 in 3 rings.

**Hits:** `ring_radius(k)` is `radius() * k / rings()`; `ring_reach_seconds(k)` is 0 for ring 1 and `BricksWave.RING_SECONDS + (k - 1) * ring_interval` for the others (0.5 and 0.65 s), the moments the drawn wave reaches them. `send_wave()` strikes ring 1 at once (`_ring_strike`, the same hits as every tier) and queues rings 2 and 3 in `pending_rings` (centre, ring, seconds left, and one shared dictionary of the instance ids this landing has hit). `step()` counts them down first thing (`_step_rings`, so they stop with the rack and run on during the wreck, like the landing) and strikes each when due: every target within its radius not yet in the dictionary. Signal `ring_hit(centre, ring, hit_count)` for every ring of a widest wave (`wave_sent` still reports the inner ring's count). `reset_for_run()` drops rings still to come. A second landing's wave has its own dictionary, so the cooldown rule is unchanged.

**Art:** `art/weapons/bricks_wave_r25_2.png` with `.json`, drawn by `tools/build_bricks_wave.py` (`build` writes every tier's sheet; `tier_waves()` reads the rings from the data): ring 1 is the tier 7 wave exactly (its frames are pixel for pixel the r8_4 sheet's), ring k sets off when ring k - 1 reaches its radius and eases out to its own in `ring_interval` (3 frames), then crumbles for 5 frames, with its own dust and chips (seed 325 + k, k times the puffs and chips). 18 frames, 0.9 s. A full frame is 2302 x 1206 art px, so a one-row sheet would be about 41 000 px wide; the sheet keeps only the 16 px high strips that hold pixels, packed into a 4096 x 928 atlas, and the record's `runs` lists each frame's strips (atlas x, atlas y, frame x, frame y, width). `BricksWave.record_for(r)` reads the record once (a static cache, also warmed in `apply_tier()` with the sheet) into one `PackedInt32Array` per frame; `_draw()` draws the frame's strips (at most about 550, one texture) only when the frame changes. `frames` and `seconds` are the wave's own (18, 0.9 s). The script's drawing loops now walk only the ring's band and the drawn pixels (the earlier sheets come out byte for byte the same).

**Cost** (headless, `tests/unit/weapons/test_bricks_widest_perf.gd`, numbers in T-498's result notes): the wave itself adds about 0.1 ms to a landing once its sheet and record are warm; with a crowd, the time goes to the minions it kills (the same per kill at tier 7), spread over the landing and the two ring steps.

**Tests:** `tests/unit/weapons/test_bricks.gd` (T-498 section: the radius 3 times tier 7's and the 3 rings; an enemy near the outer edge hit later than one near the car, at the drawn wave's times, every enemy once, a minion pushed out of the inner ring not hit again, the kaiju damaged and never pushed, each ring's count; a new run drops the rings to come; the sheet's frames in order, its strips inside the atlas, the hit times matching the sheet's record), `tests/unit/weapons/test_bricks_widest_perf.gd` (the benchmark), `tools/test_build_bricks_wave.py` (`WidestWave`: the timing, three rings drawn inner to outer, the inner ring the tier 7 wave, the pixel rules).
