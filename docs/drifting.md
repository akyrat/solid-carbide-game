# Drifting

## Summary

Drifting is the one core skill of Solid Carbide. The car's driving and drifting copy the board's older Unity prototype, including its drift curve and its camera, with a higher top speed chosen after the board's playtest of the Godot version, and the car can also reverse. The player sets the camera zoom in the pause menu. The player drives with W to go forward, S to reverse, and A and D to steer and drift.

### Prototypes so far

The board built two drift prototypes before this project:

Its driving and drifting are the reference for Solid Carbide. It also has reverse on the S key.

### Unity prototype summary

Facts from the report (Unity prototype report, sections 1 to 7). No new decisions.

- The car is a top-down 2D physics body. Every physics step (50 per second) the code sets its velocity directly, split into speed along the nose and speed across it. No forces.
- The handling values come from the car's data asset in the project files. The current code reads no driving value from the registry; the only saved setting that still matters is the camera zoom.
- W snaps the car to a cruise speed of 11 units per second (the Unity car is 1 unit long), then speed climbs in a straight line to a top speed of 27.5. Letting go coasts down in a straight line.
- S brakes in a straight line while moving forward, snaps to 11 backwards at zero, then climbs to 27.5 backwards. W always wins over S.
- A and D rotate the car at a fixed 191.9 degrees per second at every speed, including standing still.
- The drift comes from sideways speed being kept at 98% per physics step: held W + A or W + D builds a wide slide that levels off near a 75 degree drift angle at about 34 units per second.
- A coded "drift amount" ramps from 0 to 1 in 0.08 seconds and back in 0.33 seconds. With the current car values it does not change the handling; it drives the drift effects.
- The camera is orthographic, sits exactly on the car every frame, never rotates, and has no smoothing or look-ahead. Its zoom (default 14.4, half the visible height in world units) is a saved pause-menu setting.

### Godot drift prototype

The Godot copy of the Unity car (task T-012), for the board's playtest.

- **Where:** the scene `game/scenes/drift_prototype/drift_prototype.tscn`, the project's main scene, with its code in `game/scripts/driving/`. How it is built, its keys and how to run it: `game/docs/driving/drift.md`, "Drift prototype".
- **How to run it:** open `game/` in Godot 4.6 and press F5, or run `"$GODOT_BIN" --path game` from the repo root.
- **Controls:** W, S, A and D as in the Unity prototype (Unity prototype report, section 2). Tab opens the tuning panel, V switches the view. No jump, arrow keys or gamepad.
- **Ground:** flat and empty, like the Unity Grass stage, with a grid (a thin line every unit, a stronger one every 5 units) and a marker at the start point.
- **Movement:** the Unity prototype report's section 4, step by step, at 50 physics steps per second, with the Unity values (report, section 5). The GUT tests check it against the report's numbers. One difference: the comparison that decides whether W snaps to cruise speed (and S to reverse cruise speed) ignores rounding errors below 0.000000001 units per second. Without it, at some headings the forward speed reads back a hair under cruise speed and W would snap to cruise speed on every step instead of climbing to top speed.
- **Boost signal:** the car signals each time W makes it jump up to cruise speed, with the speed before the jump and whether W was just pressed. During a held W + A or W + D drift the slide pulls the forward speed under cruise speed, so the jump happens on most steps of the drift too (with W not just pressed).
- **Scale and camera:** 16 pixels per unit, the scale of the placeholder car sheets. The camera sits exactly on the drawn car, never rotates and has no smoothing or look-ahead. Its zoom is Unity's orthographic size, half the visible height in units.
- **View switch:** the physics always runs in the flat top-down world. V, or the panel's "Isometric view" toggle, switches only the drawing: flat top-down with the flat placeholder sheet, or a standard 2:1 isometric projection (screen = (x - y, (x + y) / 2)) of the same world with the isometric sheet. It can be switched mid-drive; the car's position, speed and rotation do not change.
- **Collisions (task T-033):** the car's 1 by 1 unit body bounces off walls, buildings, the map's edge and boulders, and drives through enemies without bouncing. How hard it bounces is a tuning panel slider, "Wall bounce", in a "Collisions" group at the bottom of the panel: the share of its speed into the wall the car keeps, bouncing back (0 = no bounce, 1 = full bounce); the Unity prototype has no bounce. Since task T-435 (Decisions, "The car bounces off walls") the bounce wins over W: after a hit, W held with the nose towards the wall no longer snaps the car back to cruise speed (which kept it pressed against the wall) but speeds it up at a steady rate, so the car first bounces away, then W brings it back; turning away or letting go of W ends that at once, and W is never switched off. The bounce grows with the hit speed up to a cap. A wall hit also ends the drift in progress (Decisions, "A wall hit ends the drift", task T-438): `car.gd` clears the model's `is_drifting` on the hit step, so the trick detector pays the drift once, for the time up to the hit, and a player still holding the keys starts a new drift on the next step; in a run the crash pays it first, then the 50% combo cut, as for any crash. Proposals for the playtest, on the same panel group: "Wall bounce" 0.4 (was 0.25), "Wall bounce: hardest hit" 16.5 units/s (the top speed) and "Wall bounce: W pull-back" 12 units/s per second: a head-on hit at cruise speed bounces about 0.9 units away, at top speed or faster about 1.75. The prototype has a test room for trying it: a few walls and a boulder 3 units across. The car also signals each hit (for the combo) and can be knocked back by other code (minions, meteors). Details, the collision layers and the knockback function: `game/docs/driving/collisions.md`, "Car collisions".
- **Tricks (task T-079):** the car finds the three tricks (Decisions) in its own driving without changing how it drives, and signals each one with its id for the combo and the popups. "Drifting" is the Unity definition (W or S held together with A or D); any step without it is a break. An extended drift is one whole unbroken drift, paid once when it ends (since T-246; it paid every full second before). A 360 is each full turn of the heading in one direction during one unbroken drift; turning back the other way or a break starts the count again. An alternate drift is each switch between a forward drift (W) and a backward drift (S) with the same steering side, within limits the board tunes in the tuning panel's "Tricks" group: the switch from one drift to the other may take at most 0.5 seconds, both drifts must last at least 0.3 seconds (the trick counts when the new one reaches it), and A or D must stay held on the same side during the switch. These three values are proposals. The tuning panel's readout shows the last trick. The signal, the trick ids and the details: `game/docs/driving/tricks.md`, "Tricks".
- **Camera options (task T-123):** the two camera options (Decisions, 2026-10-13), both off by default; with both off the camera is exactly as above. They work the same in the drift prototype and in the run (also a run started from the main menu), and are saved with the zoom in the player's settings. **Camera smoothing:** each physics step the camera closes 1 - e^(-8 x step time) of its gap to the drawn car (rate 8 per second), so it trails a moving car by about its speed / 8 (about 4 units at the top speed of 33 units per second, under a third of the default half-height of 14.4) and catches up within about half a second once the car stops. **Dynamic camera:** the zoom's target is the min zoom at a standstill and the max zoom at the car's top forward speed, in a straight line in between (half speed: halfway), using the car's speed in any direction as a share of its top forward speed (capped at 1), so a fast slide or a fast reverse also zooms out. The zoom eases toward the target at a rate of 3 per second (about 63% of the way in a third of a second, 95% in one second), so it never jumps. While it is on it replaces the slider's zoom; moving the slider while it is on shows that zoom at once (so the board can see the value it is about to set as min or max), and the camera eases back to the speed's zoom once the car moves. Turning it off goes straight back to the slider's zoom. **Min and max:** proposed 11 (zoomed in at a standstill) and 17 (zoomed out at top speed), around the default 14.4 and within the slider's 6 to 20; the board sets them in playtests with the pause screen's buttons (task T-124), which take the slider's current zoom. The two rates are proposals in one place in the code. Settings keys, the API for the pause screen and where the saved min and max can be read: `game/docs/hud-and-menus/camera.md`, "Camera options".
- **Camera follows the car's height (task T-228; the decision is in Decisions, 2026-10-21):** the camera's position is the ground part plus a height part. The ground part follows the car's map position as before, with the camera options (smoothing, dynamic camera). It is used in the run on the elevation map, the drive-only elevation sample scene and the elevation test scene; the drift prototype has no heights.
- **Tuning panel (Tab):** one slider per setting in the report's section 8 list except off-road damping and the physics rate (cruise speed, top speed multiplier, time to top speed, coast slowdown, turn speed, steering sensitivity, base sideways grip, drift grip at low and high speed, drift speed reference, drift enter and exit rates), plus the camera zoom (6 to 20, starting at 14.4), and the side slip out of a drift's four sliders in their own group (task T-278; "Side slip out of a drift (task T-278)" below). Nothing is saved between runs.

### Aerial tricks (task T-219)

The air 180 and air 360 (Decisions, "Aerial tricks"), built in `game/scripts/driving/trick_detector.gd` from the car's heading and its airborne state; how it is built is in `game/docs/driving/tricks.md`, "Tricks". The detector only reads the driving. Two changes to the driving in the air come from the board (below): no drifting in the air, and faster turning in the air. Ground driving is unchanged. Every point below is a proposal for the board's playtest.

- **No drifting in the air** (Decisions, "Aerial tricks", board 2026-10-21, after playing T-219): from the first step that starts with the car in the air, the driving model keeps the car's velocity (speed and direction on the map) exactly as it was at takeoff. There is no speeding up, braking, steering of the path, grip or drift. W, S, A and D only spin the body: A and D at the air turn rate, and W and S alone do nothing. The car is not drifting in the air (its drift amount fades as whenever it stops drifting), so there is no drift smoke or tyre marks, no drift likes, no LONG DRIFT! and no drift sound, and the exhaust flamethrower, which fires while drifting, is off in the air too.
  - **Not changed:** the takeoff step is still a ground step. A wall or building side met in the air still bounces the car, and an enemy's knockback still pushes it, as before; neither is driving.
  - **Landing:** driving resumes on the step after the landing, from the heading the car landed with and the velocity it kept.
    - Landing straight on, it simply drives on.
    - Landing at an angle to its path, the part of its speed along the new nose becomes its forward speed. The part across the nose is a sideways slide that fades with the normal grip: 0.98 is kept per step when not drifting, so about a third of it is left after a second. In effect it lands into a drift-like slide.
    - With W held, a forward speed under cruise snaps up to cruise along the new nose, as W always does.
    - Landing backwards (more than 90 degrees off its path) with W held, the car snaps to cruise speed forward along its new nose, which is a sudden turnaround. Holding A or D with W or S on landing is a normal drift from that step.

- **What counts:** only heading turned on physics steps that end with the car in the air (from the takeoff step to the step before landing). Turning on the ground, before the takeoff or after the landing, counts nothing, and turns in two airtimes don't add up. The turn is counted in one direction; turning back starts a new count, and the airtime keeps its largest one, so a 180 followed by a small wiggle back still counts. Letting go of A or D in the air keeps the count. W makes no difference (steering turns the car with or without it).
- **Paid on landing:** the tricks are given on the step the car lands, not mid-air, so the turn must be finished before landing, and the popup shows as the car lands. A run that resets mid-air (a new car) forgets the airtime.
- **A 360 counts only the 360, not its 180 too.** The airtime's turn is paid in half turns: 180 gives AIR 180!, 360 gives AIR 360!, **540 gives AIR 360! and AIR 180!**, **720 gives two AIR 360!**.
- **Worth:** `air_one_eighty` adds 0.25 to the combo (as much as a drift 360) and `air_three_sixty` 0.5 (twice the turn, as much as an alternate drift, the hardest ground trick), in `game/data/combo.json`, `trick_add`.
- **A drift 360 that spans the jump: each spin pays once** (Decisions, "Aerial tricks", board 2026-10-21; this replaces the first build's independent counting). Turning on a step that ends in the air counts only for the aerial tricks and never towards the drift's FULL 360!, so an air spin with W held pays AIR 360! (or AIR 180!) and no FULL 360!.
  - **At takeoff**, since there is no drifting in the air, the airtime is a break in the drift. A drift 360 in progress starts again from zero, and a drift in progress ends there and pays (T-246). A drift segment ends too, for the alternate drift's switch timing. After landing, a drift starts counting from the landing. (Before "no drifting in the air", a drift 360 in progress was kept through the airtime.)
- **Faster turning in the air** (Decisions, "Aerial tricks"; the board chose this after the first build found the 360 out of reach): in the air the car turns `air_turn_multiplier` times faster than on the ground. This is a tuning value in `game/scripts/driving/car_tuning.gd`, with a slider in the drift prototype's tuning panel ("In the air"). **Halved in T-269** (Decisions, "Slower air turn, upgradable", board 2026-10-22): the base is now **1.25**, which is 239.9 degrees a second (4.8 per physics step) in the air; the first build had 2.5 (479.75). The ground rate stays 191.9. A garage bonus (T-270) is added to the multiplier at a run's start (`air_turn_bonus`, set by `apply_air_turn_bonus()`; 0 without the upgrade). At 1.25 an air 180 takes 38 steps (0.76 s) and is reachable off the tallest rooftop (a fall counts 49 steps, 0.98 s); an air 360 takes about 76 and is not. A 360 off that rooftop becomes reachable from a total of about **1.9** (1.85 is just short, 1.95 works; 2.0 has a margin), so the upgrade's top levels should reach about 2.0 or more. The faster rate applies on every step that starts with the car in the air, so the takeoff step still turns at the ground rate.
- **What it gives on today's map** (measured in the tests): the highest drop is a height-6 rooftop onto the street, 1.0 s, of which 49 steps count as in the air.
  - **A 360 takes 38 steps (0.76 s).** A player who steers the whole fall makes it with **about 0.2 s to spare**: starting the turn 10 steps (0.2 s) late still gives AIR 360!, while 12 steps late gives only AIR 180!.
  - **A 180 takes 19 steps (0.38 s)**, half of that, so it also fits a hop off a ramp's top (about 0.7 s).
  - The most a rooftop fall can turn is about 470 degrees, so a 540 can't be done on today's map.
  - **The board, 2026-11-01:** an AIR 360! is reachable on today's map at the base air-turn speed: with enough speed a ramp throws the car far across the map, much longer in the air than a rooftop fall. The rooftop-fall numbers above stand; they are not the longest airtime.

### Airtime trick (task T-242)

The board's airtime trick (Decisions, "Airtime trick", 2026-10-21): every airtime is a trick of its own, paid on landing by how long the car was in the air. Built in `game/scripts/driving/trick_detector.gd` (how: `game/docs/driving/effects.md`, "Airtime trick"); it reads the car's airborne state like the aerial tricks and changes no driving. Its popup is "HANG TIME!" with its amount (HUD and menus, Content, T-241). The live counter while airborne is T-243's. Every number below is a proposal for the board's playtest, in `game/data/combo.json`, `airtime`.

- **Seconds** are the sum of the physics steps that end in the air (0.02 s each), so a rooftop fall of 49 steps is 0.98 s.
- **Amount** = airtime (to hundredths) times `per_second`, nothing under `min_seconds`, held at `max_add`, rounded to hundredths. It adds to the combo like any trick (held at the combo's cap; at the cap only the name shows).
- **Proposed numbers and why:**
  - `min_seconds` **0.3**: a curb or a small hop stays under it, so only a real jump pays and the popup never fires for nothing. Ramp tops and rooftop falls (about 0.7 s to 1.0 s) are well over it.
  - `per_second` **0.2**: the rooftop fall (0.98 s) pays **0.20** and a 0.7 s ramp hop 0.14. That is less than an AIR 180! (0.25), since an airtime alone asks for no steering.
  - `max_add` **0.6**: reached at 3 s, a little above an AIR 360! (0.5), so a very long fall is worth about a spin and a bit, never much more than the hardest aerial trick. Today's highest drop is 1.0 s, so the cap is not reached on today's map.
- **Next to the aerial tricks:** it pays on the same landing step, after AIR 180! or AIR 360!, as its own popup. A spin does not replace it: a 360 off a height-6 rooftop pays AIR 360! +0.5 and HANG TIME! +0.2.

### A crash pays the trick in progress (task T-315)

The Decision "A crash pays the trick in progress" (board, 2026-10-28) is in Game loop architecture, Decisions. Built in `trick_detector.gd` (`settle()`), `car.gd` (`settle_tricks()`) and `run_state.gd` (the `crashed` signal); how: `game/docs/driving/tricks.md`, "Tricks", "A crash pays the trick in progress (T-315)". A crash is anything that cuts the combo: hitting a wall, a building, a boulder, the map's edge or a side of the raised ground, or any damage to the car (minions, the kaiju, meteors).

- **The extended drift:** a drift under way when the car crashes pays its `extended_drift` at once, for the time it lasted until the crash, with its LONG DRIFT! popup and amount (`drift_pay`, as for a drift that ends normally). The keys are usually still held, so the car goes on drifting: the rest of the drift after the crash is a new drift, counted from zero, and pays when it ends (or at the next crash). Since T-438 a wall hit (not damage from enemies) also ends the drift itself, so the new drift starts on the step after the hit. A drift still shorter than `drift_pay.min_seconds` is left alone and goes on counting, as before. The XP from drifting seconds is unchanged: it is paid second by second while the drift lasts, not at its end.
- **The aerial tricks and the airtime trick:** a car in the air can crash into an obstacle or be hit by an enemy. The spins turned so far (AIR 180!, AIR 360!) and the airtime so far (HANG TIME!) pay at the crash, as if the car had landed; the rest of the airtime counts from the crash and pays on landing. An airtime still under `airtime.min_seconds` with no half turn yet is left alone.
- **Not changed, since nothing of them is in progress:** the drift 360 (FULL 360!) and the alternate drift (ALT DRIFT!) pay the moment they are complete, so a crash never cuts one that would have paid; a 360 under way is not a trick yet and keeps counting as before. Thread the needle pays a short moment after the gap is crossed; a touch of either obstacle voids the pass by its own rule (Level design, Decisions, "Thread the needle"), and other crashes don't touch it.
- **The order (proposal for the playtest, approved by the board):** the trick pays first, then the crash cuts the combo (by 50% since T-329; 80% before), so its popup shows just before COMBO BROKEN! (stacked under it). The cut then takes the same share of the trick too. The other order is a switch, `crash_pays_trick_first` in `game/data/combo.json`: `false` cuts first and then adds the trick to the cut combo, which keeps the whole trick (at the old 80% cut, a 1.7 s drift from x2.0: x1.22 paid first, x1.29 cut first) and shows COMBO BROKEN! first.
- **Feel (the agent's note for the playtest):** paying first reads as cause and effect (the drift is credited, then the crash), and both popups show at once, LONG DRIFT! under COMBO BROKEN!. The drift's amount is small (at most +0.5) and most of it is cut away, so the cost of a crash stays clear.

The HUD's drift counter (T-247) is not changed: it still shows the whole drift's seconds through a crash, while the paid part starts again from zero (a question for the playtest; HUD and menus owns it). GIF and screenshot: `art-archive/T-315/screenshots/`.

### Hype speed boost (task T-275)

The decision is in Decisions ("Hype speed boost"). Proposals for the board's playtest, kept in `game/data/combo.json` (`hype_speed`):

| Combo heat step | Combo (ComboHeat.THRESHOLDS) | Top speed above the base | Top speed (base 16.5 units/s) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | below x2 | +0% | 16.5 |
| 1 | from x2 | +8% | 17.8 |
| 2 | from x3 | +16% | 19.1 |
| 3 | from x4 up | +25% | 20.6 |

- **Why these amounts:** the first step has to be felt, and the last must not break the street's spacing. +8% (1.3 units/s) is just noticeable, +16% clearly quicker, and +25% (4.1 units/s) is the reward for holding x4 or more. The steps grow as the combo climbs, as the HUD's heat steps do.
- **Acceleration:** it rises with the top speed (my call). The ramp rate is top speed over the time to top speed, and that time (2.3 s, and the garage's acceleration upgrade) is untouched, so a hot car climbs to its higher top in the same time: about 25% quicker at x4. W's snap to cruise speed (11 units/s) is unchanged. Coasting loses a share of the top speed, so it scales the same way.
- **Easing:** the bonus moves towards the step's amount at 0.2 per second (`ease_per_second`): +8% takes 0.4 s, the full +25% 1.25 s, and it falls back at the same rate, so a combo cut by a crash slows the car over about a second, not at once.
- **What does not change:** turning, drift grip, and the drift prototype (no combo, so its top speed stays 16.5). The garage's speed upgrades multiply as before: the bonus multiplies the top speed as it stands.
- **Jumps:** a fall off the tallest rooftop (height 6) lasts 1.0 s (gravity 12) and the car keeps its takeoff speed in the air, so a jump off it carries about 16.5 units at the base top speed and about 20.6 units at the top step (4.1 units farther), if the car has reached top speed on the roof. Ramps launch farther the same way. Challenge distances (Level design) were made at the base speed, so a hot car only lands farther.
- Tests: `game/tests/unit/test_hype_speed.gd`.

### Side slip out of a drift (task T-278)

The board's "little side slip out of a drift" (Decisions). The values are **proposals for the board's own playtest**, set with sliders in the drift prototype's tuning panel. How it is built: `game/docs/driving/drift.md`, "Side slip out of a drift (T-278)".

- **How a drift ended before:** the car already slides a lot. Its sideways speed fades by only 2% every physics step, drifting or not (the Unity values), so when a drift ends the car is still about 65 degrees sideways and straightens over a second or so as its path swings round onto its nose. What stopped dead was the car's **turn**: the moment A or D was let go, the body went from turning at 192 degrees a second to not turning at all, in one step. Nothing carried on that the player had to catch.
- **What it does now:** when a drift ends on the ground because A or D is let go, the car's tail keeps swinging out a little: the body keeps turning the drift's way for a moment, so the slide grows a touch before it settles. The extra turn starts at a share of the normal turn rate and fades to nothing on its own; a touch of the other key (D after a left drift, A after a right one) catches it four times faster, and the player then steers straight. Doing nothing, it settles by itself: the whole extra turn after a full drift is about 14 degrees, a little over half of one of the car sprite's 16 directions, so it is never a spin-out. Everything else (speed, grip, the drift amount behind the smoke and marks, the tricks and their payouts) is unchanged.
- **Only after a real slide:** the slip grows with how sideways the car was when the drift ended (with the square of the drift angle, full from 60 degrees), so a quick steering touch barely slips. It only carries on a turn that was swinging the tail out, never one that was already straightening the car. Letting go of W while still steering (as in the alternate drift) has no slip, since the car is still turning.
- **Not in the air:** a slip stops when the car leaves the ground, and a drift that ends in the air or on landing has none (Decisions, "No drifting in the air").
- **The four sliders and their defaults** (group "Out of a drift: side slip"; the Unity car has no slip):
  - **Strength**, 0.35 of the turn rate (67 degrees a second) for a full drift. Small enough to read as a wobble, not a second turn. 0 turns it off.
  - **Length**, 0.4 seconds to fade with no input. Long enough to notice and catch, short enough that a player who ignores it is straight again almost at once.
  - **Catch**, 4 times faster with counter-steer: caught within a tenth of a second, so a quick tap is enough and feels decisive.
  - **Full at drift angle**, 60 degrees: a held drift settles at about 68, so every proper drift gets the full slip and small corrections don't.
- **GIFs** (in `art-archive/T-278/screenshots/`): `drift_exit_slip_prototype.gif` (the drift prototype) and `drift_exit_slip_run.gif` (a run), each with a drift with the slip off, one left alone and one caught with counter-steer, with a caption showing the slip and the drift angle; stills `prototype_slip_off_released.png`, `prototype_slip_left_alone.png`, `run_slip_caught.png`.

### Rev Charge (task T-460)

The decision is in Decisions ("Driving power-ups"); the tier numbers are above. How it is built: `game/docs/driving/drift.md`, "Rev Charge (T-460)".

- **What it does:** with Rev Charge owned, holding W and S together charges a boost; releasing S fires it. The charge fills in the tier's charge time (1.6 / 1.3 / 1.0 / 0.7 s). The boost lifts the car at once to its top speed × the tier's multiplier (×1.5 / 1.6 / 1.75 / 1.9; from a top speed of 16.5 units/s that is 24.8 / 26.4 / 28.9 / 31.4) and holds it there for the tier's boost time (0.8 / 1.0 / 1.2 / 1.5 s); then the car slows back to its top speed on its own over a second or two. The top speed it multiplies includes the hype speed boost, so a hot car boosts harder.
- **Without it:** W+S drives exactly as before (W wins over S).
- **The proposals for the playtest** (Driving & Drift Agent's calls):
  - **While charging, the car slows to cruise speed** (brakes from top speed at the normal ramp rate, about 0.8 s, then holds cruise, 11 units/s) **and keeps driving**: steering and drifting work as with W alone. Reason: the car is never parked in the middle of a run full of enemies, and a charge can be built inside a drift (the tiers' reasons count on charging mid-drift); the slowdown is the price of the charge and makes the release feel like a kick.
  - **A partial charge fires a partial boost:** the boost's extra speed and its length both scale with how full the charge was. Below a quarter of a full charge nothing fires, so a brief W+S overlap while switching keys never boosts.
  - **Letting go of W first** (S still held) drops the charge with no boost: the car then brakes or reverses on S as usual.
  - **Drifting:** charging works mid-drift (above), and the boost holds its speed through a drift; it is the same forward push as W's, so the drift's grip and the tricks are unchanged.
  - **The air:** the charge does not grow in the air but is kept; releasing S in the air drops it (no boost: in the air the car keeps its takeoff speed, Decisions, "No drifting in the air"). A boost already running keeps its clock in the air and holds its speed again on landing if time is left.
  - **Walls:** a wall, building, boulder or ground-side hit ends a running boost (as it ends the drift since T-438); a charge in progress is kept.
  - **The wreck:** the controls go dead, so the charge and any running boost are dropped, with no boost.
- **The placeholder effect** (drawn in code until the art ticket replaces it): while charging, a round glow at the car's rear, in the heat yellows and oranges, that grows with the charge and pulses once it is full; while boosting, five light blue-grey speed lines streaming back from the rear, fading as the boost runs out. Both stop when the charge or boost ends; nothing shows without the power-up.
- Screenshots and GIF: `art-archive/T-460/screenshots/`.

### Jump (task T-461)

The decision is in Decisions ("Driving power-ups"); the tier numbers are above. How it is built: `game/docs/driving/tricks.md`, "Jump (T-461)".

- **What it does:** with Jump owned, a press of Space on the ground makes a small hop. It is the car's own height (Elevation): the car leaves the ground straight up at the tier's takeoff speed (5 / 6 / 7 / 8 units/s), falls with the same gravity as off a rooftop (12 units/s²) and lands. Everything a fall off a rooftop does, the hop does: the car keeps its flat speed and heading in the air (no driving in the air) and spins faster there, it meets the sides of rooftops and ramps at its height and passes over what is lower than it, and its airtime and spins pay the airtime, AIR 180 and AIR 360 tricks, so they count for the missions' airtime and air-spin goals (board, 2026-10-31).
- **How high and how long** (from the tier numbers, flat ground): about 1.0 / 1.5 / 2.0 / 2.7 units high, 0.83 / 1.0 / 1.17 / 1.33 s in the air.
- **Without it:** Space does nothing while driving, as before.
- **The proposals for the playtest** (Driving & Drift Agent's calls):
  - **Only on the ground, no double jump:** a press in the air does nothing and is not kept for the landing. Reason: the hop is a small lift, not flying; a double jump would make the tallest tier reach rooftops.
  - **The cooldown counts from the takeoff** (the tier's 2.0 / 1.7 / 1.4 / 1.1 s), in the air too; a press while it runs does nothing and is not kept. So at tier 1 the next hop is ready about 1.2 s after landing, at tier 4 as soon as the car lands.
  - **One hop per press:** holding Space does not hop again on landing.
  - **From anywhere on the ground:** a street, a rooftop or a ramp; on a ramp it adds to nothing (straight up at the takeoff speed).
- **Note on the tier reasons (for Game Data and the board):** at the base air turn rate (240 degrees a second) the tier 1 hop already fits an air 180 (200 degrees), and the tier 4 hop (320 degrees) does not quite fit an air 360 without the garage's air turn upgrade. The tier numbers are unchanged; the playtest decides.

### The Jump clears obstacles (task T-478)

The decision is in Decisions ("The Jump clears obstacles"). How it is built: `game/scripts/level/mvp_map.gd` (`obstacle_heights`, `obstacle_floor`, `update_car_obstacles`) and `game/scripts/driving/car_height.gd` (`floor_height`).

- **The rule:** every boulder and prop has a bottom and a top (the ground it stands on, up to its height). The car passes over it while its height is at the top (within 0.05 units) or above, and meets it as a wall, with the bounce, when it is lower. Walls (skyscrapers) and the map's edge have no top: they block at any height. Short buildings are platforms of 3 units: a hop that stays below 3 meets their side as before.
- **Heights** (proposals for the playtest): a boulder (the map's and the meteors') is **0.8 units** high (width stays its 3-unit diameter). A prop's collision height is its `height_units` in `creator_assets.json` times 0.75 (`PROP_HEIGHT_SCALE`, so the street props are about as low as a boulder). The hop's peak: tier 1 1.04, tier 2 1.5, tier 3 2.04, tier 4 2.67.
- **What each tier clears** (collision heights): boulder 0.8, barrier 0.9, cones 0.68, broken cars 0.71: every tier. Parked cars 1.01 and a prop of 1.1 to 1.2 (AC unit, roof vent, after the scale): tier 2 and up. Crates 1.65: tier 3 and up. Roof sign and water tank 2.5: tier 4. The street lamp (4.0), the 3-unit platforms, the skyscrapers (30) and the map's edge: no tier.
- **Timing:** a car passes an obstacle once it is at its top (within 0.05), or while it is rising, its apex clears the obstacle's top and it is already 10% of the way up from the foot (`RISE_PASS_FRACTION`). So Space pressed when the car's drawn nose looks level with the obstacle (its body is 1 unit, its sprite 3, so contact comes about 1 unit, 0.06 s at full speed, later) clears it, and a Space about 0.04 s later than that still does; later, the car meets it as a wall. The board played the first version (a 0.8 clearance, pressed 6 to 7 units short) and could not do it.
- **Never landing inside** (proposal): the car lands on the obstacle it is over, at its top, and drives along it like on a low platform; off its edge it falls to the ground. It is never put inside an obstacle by a landing (a car falling through one it passes is put on its top).

### The Polarized Steering Wheel (task T-502)

The decision is in Decisions ("Rusty Paintjob and the Polarized Steering Wheel"). How it is built: `game/docs/driving/drift.md`, "Polarized Steering Wheel (T-502)". Its card texts are T-501's (HUD and menus); its icon is a script-drawn placeholder (a steering wheel with a cyan and magenta hub) until T-503's art.

- **What it does:** with the power-up owned, letting go of W, steering, then pressing W again while the car's nose points at least 60 degrees away from where the car is moving gives a short speed burst: for 0.5 s the car's forward speed is held at least at the tier's multiplier (x1.2 / 1.3 / 1.4 / 1.45, so +20 / +30 / +40 / +45%) times the speed it carried into the press (cruise speed, 11 units/s, at the least). After a lift from near top speed and a steer of about 75 degrees (about 0.4 s), the car carries about 12.4 units/s, so the burst is about 14.8 / 16.1 / 17.3 / 17.9 units/s forward (measured in the tests; top speed is 16.5). Afterwards the car slows back to its top speed on its own, as after Rev Charge.
- **Tier 4, the shock:** every tier 4 burst also sets off Bricks' normal wave where the car is: Bricks' tier 1 wave (radius 4, damage 6, minions pushed 4 units away, the kaiju damaged and never pushed), drawn with Bricks' r4 wave sheet, whether or not the car has Bricks; owning Bricks at a higher tier (even its widest wave) does not change it. Its numbers are read from Bricks' tier 1 in the weapons data, so they follow Bricks. The garage damage bonus applies to it, as to every weapon.
- **Without it:** pressing W again after a coast does nothing new.
- **The look:** the cruise boost's motion lines, stronger: three sets side by side across the car instead of one, looping for the whole half second.
- **The proposals for the playtest** (Driving & Drift Agent's calls; numbers in `power_ups.json` with reasons):
  - **The angle:** at least 60 degrees between the nose and the direction of travel at the press (the board's start), on every tier.
  - **The coast may last 1.2 s** at most from letting go of W to pressing it again: enough to swing the nose round 180 degrees at the turn rate, short enough to read as one move. A longer coast gives no burst.
  - **S during the coast cancels it:** braking or reversing is not a coast, so S then W (the car moving backwards, nose forwards) is no burst.
  - **The car must be moving at least 2 units/s** (a standing car has no direction to turn away from), and on the ground: a press in the air does nothing.
  - **The burst's size comes from the speed carried in**, so keeping speed through the turn pays. No cooldown: each new lift, steer and press gives a new burst (and at tier 4 a new shock).
  - **Walls and the wreck:** a wall, building, boulder or ground-side hit ends a running burst (as for Rev Charge); the wreck drops it.
  - **Holding W throughout never bursts,** however far a drift turns the nose: the press of W is the trigger.
- **Where it appears:** in the mission gift's pool like the other four, and on the tutorial's missions slide with them.

- **Leaving a grind.** Space (a jump key that only works on a wall) makes a small hop with no trick, landing beside the wall; any arrow trick also carries the car off. Either way the car keeps its speed and direction along the wall, its nose turned along the wall, and doesn't land back on the same wall or count it as a jump-over.
- **Known limits:** dropping off moved the car sideways in one step, and its nose turned along the wall at once.

**The art.** T-153 made the side-flip roll with PixelLab for one direction (the car's start direction, frame 12): one `/animate-with-text-v3` call, 8 frames, palette-matched by script. The board found it flat, like flipping a 2D image. T-157 then made a sample with real depth for the same direction: a script (`game/tools/render_trick_guides.py`) draws a simple 3D model of the car rolled or pitched by any angle, and each frame is one PixelLab `/rotate` call with the real car as the reference and the drawing as the pose (15 calls, starting-image strength 450); `game/tools/build_trick_frames.py` maps the colours and centres the frames. The result: the nose pop has real depth in most frames (the 135-degree one is weak); the roll is inconsistent in its middle frames. The board hadn't chosen the next step for the roll when the prototype was stopped (a free script fix was the Project Lead's pick).
