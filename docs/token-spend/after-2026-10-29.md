# Token spend: after the HUD batch (2026-10-29)

Rerun of the token report after the first real batch went through the new tools, as `docs/token-spend/after-2026-10-28-early.md` planned. Written by the Tokens session on 2026-10-29, on the board's request. The early read stays as it was.

## Read this first

- Period covered: 2026-10-28 from 03:55:00Z (end of the early read; Greenbull's go for the HUD work) to the time of this run, 2026-10-29 shortly after 01:00Z.
- 1,415 calls (337 in two main sessions, 1,078 in their subagents), against 20,248 in the baseline. Plus 42 calls (2.1M tokens) in sessions run inside the T-325 worktree, which only this report reads (`--dir`).
- Two sessions: Greenbull (`0a239fb3`), which built, played and merged the livestream HUD batch, and Tokens (`2c104619`), which merged T-350 and built T-354 and T-355.
- Merged in the period: 16 tickets. HUD batch, 13: T-325, T-331, T-332, T-333, T-334, T-335, T-336, T-337, T-338, T-339, T-340, T-341, T-351. Tokens, 3: T-350, T-354, T-355. T-353 was closed without an agent. T-352 was still merging when this ran, so its work is counted but not its merge.
- Unlike the early read, this is product work: UI, game-loop and art tickets, built by `ui`, `game-loop` and `asset-generation` agents, with the board's playtests and quick-tuning rounds (T-351 had three). It is still one batch, not the baseline's mix of driving, level and art work.
- The guard changed in the middle of the period: T-354 (the guard refuses only real ticket writes) went live at 15:14:19Z. Failures are therefore also shown before and after that time.

## Other changes in the period, and what cannot be told apart

- The 500k auto-compact limit (board, 2026-10-28) never acted: the highest context was 291k.
- The usage limit cut both sessions off for some hours; restarted sessions begin small, which lowers average context.
- Greenbull's period includes the board's own playtests and three quick-tuning rounds on T-351; their agent calls count against the batch.
- The Tokens session's own system reminders (cross-session messages, task notifications, background checks) are 46% of its weighted context. Coordination between two Project Leads costs context, not only tool calls.
- Sessions in ticket worktrees: only T-325's folder had calls in the period (42 calls, 2.1M), added by hand to the per-ticket figure.

## What this does and does not say

Does: T-354 brought failures and hook refusals back to about the baseline. The ticket scripts (merge, status, new ticket) give short output. Tokens per merged ticket are lower (11.0M overall, 8.8M for the HUD batch) on cheaper work. Context stays well under the old peaks.

Does not: show that QA got cheaper (it got much dearer per ticket), or that subagent reports shrank in Greenbull's session. Those are the two places to look next.
