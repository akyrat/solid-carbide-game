# Weapons

## Summary

The car's weapons fire on their own, so the player only drives. The MVP has five: a simple gun the player starts every run with, which automatically shoots at enemies in range, a flamethrower out of the exhaust that fires only while drifting, a neon underglow that hurts enemies around the car, molotovs that leave fire on the ground, and a windshield wiper that sweeps enemies away.

## Decisions

- The MVP has 2 weapons: the starting gun and the exhaust flamethrower (below); since 2026-10-18, 5 (the three new weapons, below). Each level-up offers a choice between them (the level-up flow below). (2026-10-07; per level-up rather than per challenge since 2026-10-08)
- The final release has around 8 to 12 weapons, and each level-up offers 3 to choose from. (2026-10-07; per level-up since 2026-10-08)
- **Size upgrades grow on screen** (board, 2026-10-24, after playing the game build): when a picked upgrade makes a weapon bigger (the wiper, the underglow, the flame), the weapon grows to its new size with a short animation instead of jumping to it. The damage area follows what is drawn. The timing is the Weapon Behavior Agent's proposal (T-286).
- The Game Data Agent works out how weapon upgrades scale from level to level, starting from the Unity prototype's upgrade values (below). (2026-10-08)
- **Starting gun:** the player starts every run with it. It has no visible weapon on the car, only the bullets it fires. It fires only when an enemy is within range, aims automatically, and fires at set intervals. (2026-10-08)
- **Exhaust flamethrower:** fires flames out of the car's exhaust, only while the car is drifting. (2026-10-08)
- Both MVP weapons copy the behaviour of the matching weapons in the board's Unity prototype (its starting gun and its Flame Exhaust): targeting, firing, the flame's shape and what turns it on. A report on how they work there is task T-022. (2026-10-08)
- Their upgrades also copy the Unity prototype's: the same upgrade tiers and what each tier changes. (2026-10-08)
- **Level-up flow in the MVP:** each level-up offers 2 choices and the player takes 1. The player starts every run with the gun, so the first level-up offers "get the flamethrower" or "upgrade the gun"; once the player has both, each level-up offers "upgrade the gun" or "upgrade the flamethrower". (2026-10-08)
- A weapon at its last upgrade tier is no longer offered: the level-up offers the other weapon's next tier instead, and if both are at their last tier, the level-up is skipped and the run goes on. (2026-10-10)
- Where the Unity prototype's weapons behave in unexpected ways (Unity weapons report, section 7), the MVP copies them, with three fixes (2026-10-10):
  - **Copied:** the flame uses the gun's damage (2), not its own (1); the bullet's hit circle stays 2 units across, larger than it is drawn; the gun looks for a target only once per firing interval; the weapons never push enemies back; (the prototype's flame steering tier and burn-flash tier are replaced by the board's own tree, 2026-11-02); reversing while steering counts as drifting, so the flame comes on.
  - **Fixed:** damage is not rounded to whole numbers, so the flame's "hotter flame" tier always adds damage; the flame's area is the same size whichever way the car faces; a bullet that misses is removed once it has flown past the gun's range.
- The exhaust flamethrower does not switch off the drift smoke (unlike the Unity prototype): smoke and flames both show. (Since 2026-10-22 any enemy is hit when any part of it touches the drawn flame; see "A smaller exhaust flame that burns what it touches" below.) The kaiju is hit when any part of its body overlaps the flames. The starting gun's bullet size and its wait before the first shot stay as built (the Unity values). (board, 2026-10-14)
- **The flamethrower at height** (board, 2026-10-21): on a rooftop or in the air, the exhaust flames come out of the car's exhaust where the car is drawn, at its height, not somewhere else on the map; they burn what they touch at that spot. (T-206)
- **The underglow and the wiper at height** (board, 2026-10-21): the neon ring is drawn under the car at the car's height, so on a rooftop, a ramp or in the air it rises with the car instead of staying on the street; the windshield wiper's arm is lifted the same way. Their damage areas are unchanged. The molotov fire stays on the ground where the bottle landed, so it is not lifted. In the air the ring stays under the car (a proposal for the playtest). (T-225)
- **Three new weapons** (board, 2026-10-18), gained at a level-up like the flamethrower:
  - **Neon underglow:** neon lights under the car that make a circular area around it. Enemies inside take damage every second, and an enemy takes damage as soon as it enters the area.
  - **Molotovs:** thrown from the car at random, each one makes a circular area on fire where it lands. Enemies in a fire area burn and take damage.
  - **Windshield wiper:** one wiper in front of the car that sweeps in a semicircle, like a windshield wiper. Enemies it hits take damage and are knocked back. It is the only weapon that pushes enemies (the "never push enemies back" rule above is about the gun and the flamethrower).
  - They join the MVP, which then has 5 weapons (6 with Bricks, below). Each has 8 tiers like the gun and the flamethrower: getting it is tier 1, then 7 upgrades. Their numbers and what each tier changes are proposals by the agents, judged in the board's playtest.
  - Answers to the goal runner's questions (board, 2026-10-18): an enemy burns only while it is inside a molotov's fire area, not after it leaves. The windshield wiper sweeps all the time; it damages the kaiju but pushes only minions. The garage's damage bonus multiplies the damage of every weapon, the three new ones included. The starting gun's level-up card shows a small pixel-art gun.
- **A smaller exhaust flame that burns what it touches** (board, 2026-10-22, after playtests; replaces "a minion is in the flames when its centre is" of 2026-10-14): when the flamethrower is first picked up (tier 1) its flame is drawn 4 to 5 times smaller than today (a proposal of about a quarter of today's length and width), growing with its size tiers as before; it is drawn behind the car and behind the enemies, never over them; and its damage area is the flame as drawn, so a minion takes damage as soon as any part of it touches the visible flame (the kaiju as before, any overlap). (T-267)
- **A stronger wiper push** (board, 2026-10-22, after playtests): the windshield wiper's first level (tier 1, when it is picked up) pushes a minion back about twice the car's size; read as twice the car's length, 6 units (the car is 1 unit wide and 3 long), against 1.5 today. The later tiers keep their multipliers, so they grow from the new base. (T-266)
  - **Level-up offers with five weapons:** each level-up still offers 2 choices, now two different ones drawn at random from every choice available (getting a weapon the car doesn't have, or the next tier of one it has).
- **The first gift pack can hold any weapon** (board, 2026-10-28; replaces "The first level-up still offers 'get the flamethrower' or 'upgrade the gun'" above): the first level-up draws its two choices at random from every choice available, like every later level-up, not only the gun and the flamethrower. (T-321)
- **Bricks** (board, 2026-10-28): a new weapon. The car carries a load of bricks; each time the car lands on the ground after being in the air, from any height, a powerful impact wave goes out around it and damages the enemies it reaches. In the first build the wave is the same whatever the height of the fall. Its card makes it sound useless, so the player is tempted to try a jump and find out: the board's direction for the description is "Bricks - A bunch of bricks. No real use, other than making the car heavier." with a hint to jump. Text: T-322; numbers: T-323; the weapon: T-324; art: T-325.
- **Weapons hit only enemies on the car's own level** (board, 2026-10-28; replaces "Their damage areas are unchanged" in "The underglow and the wiper at height" above, for damage): on a map with rooftops and ramps, a weapon damages only the enemies on the same height level as the weapon's hit: the car's level for the gun, the flamethrower, the underglow, the wiper and Bricks, and the fire's level for a molotov. An enemy on the street is not hit from a rooftop above it, and the other way round. How a ramp's in-between heights and the kaiju count are the agents' proposals for the playtest. (T-326)
- **The gun one-shots minions and grows in bullets, speed and targets** (board, 2026-10-30; replaces the gun's tier plan in "The starting gun" below and answers the open question on upgrading how often it fires): from tier 1 one bullet kills a minion. Its upgrades go to how many bullets it fires and how fast, in this order: **+1 bullet** twice, **faster attack** twice, then at the halfway upgrade it **targets up to 3 enemies at once** (it has 3 bullets by then); then **+1 bullet** twice and **faster attack** twice, and at its last upgrade it **targets up to 5 enemies at once**. That makes 10 upgrades (tiers 2 to 11) against today's 7; the range and pass-through tiers go. What "targets up to N" means against today's closest-first spread, and the speeds, are the agents' proposals for the playtest. (T-408) (Superseded by the board's own upgrade trees, 2026-11-02, below: the gun has 8 tiers and no multi-target.)
- **The neon underglow kills a minion in 2 ticks and pushes from tier 1** (board, 2026-10-30): each tick deals half a minion's maximum health at tier 1, so 2 ticks kill it, and from tier 1 it knocks enemies back a little (today it never pushes). How far, and whether the push grows with the tiers, are proposals. (T-409)
- **Weapons during the wreck** (board, 2026-10-30, audit): while the car is wrecked (Game loop architecture, Decisions, "The wreck decides the loss at its end"), damage already on the map keeps going (molotov fires, burning enemies) and the neon underglow keeps ticking; the bricks still go off if the wrecked car lands from the air. Nothing new is fired: no gun, no molotov throws, no wiper, no exhaust flamethrower. Built in T-414. Proposals for the playtest (T-414, not the board's yet): a bullet or a molotov bottle already in the air carries on and lands; a gun volley still queued is dropped; burns the flamethrower already put on enemies keep burning, and damaging (afterburn, T-497), until they run out; and the weapons stop at once when the run ends, even in the middle of a step (a kaiju kill ends the run, and nothing later in that step damages anything).
- **Molotovs land where enemies can be** (board, 2026-10-30, audit): a molotov whose landing spot is inside a building or off the map picks another spot. Built in T-414. Proposals for the playtest: a short building counts as ground (a platform the car can drive onto), so a bottle may land on it; a bottle tries up to 30 spots and, if none fits, lands at the car.
- **The board's own upgrade trees** (board, 2026-11-02, made in the Project Lead's Upgrade Tree Workshop page; built in T-495 to T-499). Tier 1 is getting the weapon; each later tier is one level-up card, in this order:
  - **Gun** (8 tiers, no more multi-target): 2 +1 bullet (Extra Bullet), 3 +1 bullet (Another Extra Bullet), 4 fire rate ×1.2 (Quicker Trigger), 5 fire rate ×1.2 (Quicker Trigger II), 6 +1 bullet (Bullet Bundle), 7 fire rate ×1.2 (Quicker Trigger III), 8 fire rate ×4 (Exposure for Everyone). The volley keeps going at one target: each bullet, as it leaves, aims at the current target if it is still alive, otherwise at the closest living enemy; a bullet already flying at an enemy that dies just before it lands is fine (the gun's single-target stream, T-408, now for the whole run).
  - **Hot Take Exhaust:** 2 size ×1.2 (Wider Burn), 3 damage ×1.2 (Hotter Burn), 4 size ×1.3 (Wider Burn II), 5 +1 s burn (Flammable Material), 6 size ×1.3 (Wider Burn III), 7 +1 s burn (Hot Stuff), 8 always on (Lit 24/7). The burn is real afterburn: an enemy that leaves the flame keeps taking the flame's damage every tick for its burn time (until now the burn time was only the orange flash).
  - **Hot Drop:** 2 fire radius ×1.2 (Bigger Blaze), 3 throw interval ×0.8 (Quick Throw), 4 +1 molotov (Another one), 5 +1 molotov (ANOTHER ONE), 6 throw interval ×0.7 (Quick Fire), 7 fire radius ×1.3 (Bigger Blaze II), 8 +1 molotov (MOREEEE): 4 molotovs per throw at the top.
  - **Bricks:** 2 radius ×1.4 (Wider Wave), 3 damage ×1.5 (Harder Landing), 4 knockback ×1.5 (Shove Factor), 5 radius ×1.5 (Wider Wave II), 6 damage ×1.5 (Harder Landing II), 7 knockback ×1.5 (Shove Factor II), 8 radius ×3 on top of the rest (Widest Wave): a new wave with 2 extra outer crash rings around the usual one, a wave travelling from the inner ring to the outer ones for a sense of huge impact; each ring hits enemies as the wave reaches it. Tuned in the playtest.
  - **Wiper Blade** (no damage upgrades): 2 length ×1.2 (Longer Blade), 3 sweep speed ×1.25 (Faster Sweep), 4 knockback ×1.5 (Hard Shove), 5 length ×1.3 (Even Longer Blade), 6 sweep speed ×1.3 (Faster Sweep II), 7 length ×1.3 (Longest Blade!), 8 a second wiper at the back of the car (Back Wiper): an exact copy of the front one with all its upgrades, sweeping behind the car, with the same blade art.
  - **Neon Underglow:** unchanged.
  - Card text: the board's titles stay exactly as written; the Narrative Theme Agent writes the missing card texts and corrects numbers that would be wrong, and leaves the board's other wording alone.
- **Shorter trees: 5 tiers** (board, 2026-11-03, for the 4-minute run, Game loop architecture, Decisions, "A 4-minute run"; replaces the 8-tier trees above, which stay as the record of the board's tiers): each weapon has **5 tiers**. Tier 1 is getting it (the gun at the start; any other weapon arrives straight at **tier 2** when first picked), and the old tiers 2 to 7 are merged in pairs, the same kind of upgrade together, their numbers multiplied, so tier 5 is exactly the old tier 8's power. Mixed pairs are fine (board). The exact stacked numbers stay, not rounded (board). Tier 2 / 3 / 4 / 5:

## Content

### The 5-tier trees as built (task T-520)

A tier with two kinds of upgrade has two entries with the same tier number; all of a tier's effects apply when it is reached. **The tier lists further down in this Content (the 8-tier trees, the T-166 and T-496 numbers, tier 8 finales, "tiers 2 to 8") are the record of the 8-tier build; where they differ, this section is the game.** Tier 2 / 3 / 4 / 5:

- **Gun:** +2 bullets (3 a volley) / fire rate x1.44 / +1 bullet (4) and fire rate x1.2 / fire rate x4 (final: 4 bullets, about 0.23 s between volleys, the old tier 8).
- **Hot Take Exhaust (flamethrower):** size x1.56 / damage x1.2 (2.4) and size x1.3 / +2 s burn / always on.
- **Neon Underglow:** radius x1.5 / damage x2.25 (4.5) / tick interval x0.5 (the docs' number; 0.75 x 0.67 stacked is 0.5025) / radius x1.3 (**placeholder**, the board has not accepted it as the finale; marked in the data's `_comment`).
- **Hot Drop (molotovs):** fire radius x1.56 / +2 molotovs (3 a throw) / throw interval x0.56 (1.68 s) / +1 molotov (4 a throw).
- **Windshield Wiper:** length x1.56 / sweep speed x1.625 / knockback x1.5 and length x1.3 / the back wiper.
- **Bricks:** radius x2.1 (8.4) / damage x2.25 (13.5) / knockback x2.25 / Widest Wave: the radius times 3 (25.2), 3 rings at the tier 4 radius (8.4), twice and three times it.

### Garage damage bonus

The garage's damage bonus upgrade (Garage design) is applied at the start of every run: its percentage multiplies the damage of the gun and the flamethrower, on top of each weapon's own tier (the two multiply). Level 0 changes nothing. Built in T-062.

### Unity prototype weapons

Facts from the Unity prototype weapons report (sections 1 to 5). No new decisions.

- The prototype's starting gun is its "Simple Gun" (`SimpleGunWeapon`, data asset `Weapon_MachineGun`): the only weapon on the car when a run starts.
- The gun checks for living enemies within 12 units of the car's centre every 1.26 seconds and fires one bullet at the closest. Bullets fly straight from the car's centre at 15 units per second, deal 2 damage, and are removed on their first hit. Bullets that miss are never removed.
- The Flame Exhaust is on while the car is drifting (W or S held with A or D) at 0.85 units per second or more, and for 0.2 seconds after. It damages enemies inside a rectangle behind the car that starts each drift at half size and reaches full size (about 2.6 wide and 3.25 deep) after 1 second of drifting.
- An enemy entering the flame takes 2 damage at once, and every enemy inside takes 2 more every 0.2 seconds. The flame takes this damage from the gun's stats; its own asset's damage (1) is not used.
- Each weapon has 8 tiers: getting it is tier 1, and each later pick applies the next of 7 upgrades. The gun gets more bullets, faster fire, a wider range and, last, bullets that pierce the first enemy. The flame gets a bigger area, more damage, a longer burn flash, faster car steering and, last, an always-on flame.
- The prototype's level-up offers 3 choices drawn at random from power-ups, new weapons and the next tiers of owned weapons. A weapon at tier 8 is no longer offered, and when nothing is left the only choice is 4 coins.
- The report lists every value with its source, the differences from Solid Carbide's level-up flow, notes on porting to Godot, and open questions for the board: [weapons/unity-weapons-report.md](weapons/unity-weapons-report.md).

### The starting gun (built in task T-041)

What the built gun does in the game. It follows Decisions and the Unity prototype weapons report, with the board's T-408 rework and, since T-496, the board's own 8-tier tree (Decisions, "The gun one-shots minions and grows in bullets, speed and targets"); the numbers are T-040's and T-408's ([../game/data/weapons.json](../game/data/weapons.json)). How the code is put together: `game/docs/weapons/shared.md` and `game/docs/weapons/starting-gun.md`.

- Every run starts with the gun at tier 1 and no other weapon; a new run puts it back to tier 1 and removes any bullets in flight. Nothing is drawn on the car, only the bullets.
- Every 1.26 seconds (at tier 1) the gun looks for living enemies whose centre is within 12 units of the car's centre. With none, it does nothing until the next check; with some, it fires a volley. It checks only at those moments, so an enemy that comes into range just after a check is shot at the next one.
- **Targeting rule** (T-408, now for the whole run since T-496, a proposal for the playtest): the gun has one target at a time at every tier (no Multi-Target Mode any more). The enemies in range are sorted closest first, and the volley's bullets leave 0.05 seconds apart, each at the closest enemy in range that is alive and not already doomed: an enemy counts as doomed when the gun's bullets in flight at it carry at least its health. As each bullet leaves it aims at its queued target if that is alive and not doomed, otherwise at the closest living enemy. So against minions (one bullet each) bullet 1 goes to the closest, bullet 2 to the next closest and so on, and each +1 bullet upgrade kills one more minion a volley; a tough enemy (the kaiju) takes the whole volley. When every enemy in range is doomed, the bullet goes at its first target anyway. A bullet already flying at an enemy that dies just before it lands is fine.
  - Each bullet leaves the car's centre aimed at its target's centre at that moment, with no leading, and flies straight on.
- Bullets fly at 15 units per second and hit an enemy whose body comes within 1 unit of the bullet's centre, dealing 4 damage through the enemy's damage function (a minion's health, so one bullet kills a minion from tier 1; the garage's damage bonus only adds to it). A bullet is gone after its first hit, or once it has flown as far as the gun's range without hitting anything. Bullets never push enemies and pass through walls and boulders.
- The gun targets every damageable enemy, not only minions, so the kaiju will be shot as soon as it exists.
- The upgrade tiers (2 to 8, the board's own tree, T-496; Decisions, "The board's own upgrade trees") change the gun in the board's order: +1 bullet at tiers 2 and 3 (2, then 3 bullets a volley), fire rate x1.2 at tiers 4 and 5 (1.26 s at tier 1, then 1.06 and 0.893 s), +1 bullet at tier 6 (4 bullets), fire rate x1.2 at tier 7 (0.754 s) and fire rate x4 at tier 8 (Exposure for Everyone: about 0.234 s between volleys, 4 bullets each, so roughly 17 bullets a second). The range stays 12 units, bullets never pass through an enemy, and the target count stays 1. "x1.2" multiplies the fire rate by 1.2, as the level-up cards say; the interval is 1.2 s divided by the rate, plus the prototype's 0.06 s. The level-ups offer the gun's upgrades until tier 8 (the level-up offers, below).
- **The kaiju** takes the same 4 damage a bullet. With the gun alone that roughly halves its fight against T-039's numbers: about 158 s at tier 1 (was 315) and a few seconds at tier 8 (the kaiju's 500 health, 125 bullets; flame and garage bonus shorten it further). A proposal for the playtest; Enemies' kaiju numbers are unchanged.
- The gun stops while the run is paused and once the run has ended.
- The bullets are a small cyan neon ball with a white-hot core, pulsing in 2 frames, drawn about half a unit above the ground (art: T-071, drawn by script, `game/art/effects/bullet.png`).

### The exhaust flamethrower (built in task T-042)

What the built flamethrower does in the game. It follows Decisions (the prototype's Flame Exhaust with the board's fixes) and takes every number from [../game/data/weapons.json](../game/data/weapons.json). How the code is put together: `game/docs/weapons/flamethrower.md`, "The exhaust flamethrower".

- **Owning it:** a run starts without it. It is one of the car's weapons next to the gun, gained at a level-up (the level-up offers, below), which also applies its upgrade tiers. A playtest switch (`game/data/playtest.json`, `flamethrower_at_start`) gives it to the car at the start of every run.
- **On and off:** on while the car is drifting (W, or S, held with A or D: the car's own drift state) at 0.85 units per second or more, and for 0.2 seconds after the drift ends. Driving straight, steering while standing still, or drifting slower than that never lights it. From tier 8 it is always on. It stops while the run is paused.
- **Area (T-267):** the flame as it is drawn, at every moment: the outline of the pixels of the flame art's current picture (its direction and flicker frame), at the drawn size, from the exhaust, pointing back. There is no separate rectangle any more. At tier 1 the flame is a quarter of its old size (size factor 0.625, was 2.5): about 30 screen pixels long at full size, 15 at the start of a drift. Each drift starts it at half size; it grows to full size over 1 second of drifting (always full size at tier 8). It is the same size on the screen whichever way the car faces.
- **Why the area and the art differed before (T-267):** the area was a rectangle from numbers in the data (3.25 units deep, 2.6 wide at full size, lined up with the car in the flat world), while the drawing was the 95-pixel plume scaled by the same size factor, but with no link to the rectangle: the plume was about twice as long as the rectangle's depth on the screen, the rectangle started 0.175 units behind the car's centre and the drawing 0.42, and a rectangle is not a teardrop. A minion counted only by its centre, so the flame could cover most of its body without burning it.
- **Where it starts (T-267, a proposal for the playtest):** the car's rear bumper, 1.2 units behind its centre (it was 0.42, under the car's body). At a quarter of the size the whole flame would otherwise sit under the car and not show.
- **Who is hit (T-267):** an enemy is hit when any part of its body touches the flame: its body circle (the minions' 0.5 units, the kaiju's own size) against the flame's outline. It replaces "a minion is inside when its centre is" (board, 2026-10-14; Decisions, 2026-10-22).
- **Damage and afterburn (T-497):** an enemy touching the flame catches fire: it takes the flame's damage (2 at tier 1) at once, turns orange with small flames on it, and then takes the flame's damage again every 0.2 seconds (the tick) for as long as it burns. While it touches the flame its burn stays full; once it leaves, it keeps burning, and taking damage, for the burn time (0.38 seconds at tier 1, longer with the burn tiers), then the tint and flames go. It burns on even when the flame goes out and while the car is wrecked. Touching the flame again refreshes the burn time without an extra hit, and an enemy never takes more than one flame hit per tick, inside the flame or burning. Each hit is the flame's damage at that moment (after its upgrades and the garage bonus). No limit on how many it hits, no push. It burns every enemy, through their damage function. Until T-497 the burn time was only the orange flash, and an enemy took damage only while inside the flame.
- **Tiers** (the board's own tree, T-496): tiers 2, 4 and 6 make the flame, drawn and hit area, bigger (x1.2, x1.3, x1.3: size factor 0.75, 0.975, 1.2675); tier 3 multiplies the damage by 1.2 (2.4 per hit, not rounded); tiers 5 and 7 add +1 s burn each (the burn time goes 0.38, 1.38, 2.38 s; T-497 makes it real afterburn, damage that keeps ticking after an enemy leaves the flame); tier 8 keeps it always on at full size. There is no steering tier any more.
- **Look:** a pixel-art flame plume (orange, yellow and a pale core) out of the exhaust in 32 directions, flickering between 2 frames, drawn behind the car and behind every enemy (one depth step under the car's, so also under enemies standing at the same depth; over the ground, rooftops and buildings), on the street, on rooftops and in the air, at the car's height. The drift smoke still shows with the flames (board, 2026-10-14). Enemies it burns show small flames on them (`burning_flames.png`). Art: T-071, from a PixelLab sample the board approved (`game/art/effects/exhaust_flame_sheet.png`).

### Numbers for the three new weapons (proposal, task T-166)

Proposals by the Game Data Agent for the board's playtest; the board judges and asks for changes. Numbers: [../game/data/weapons.json](../game/data/weapons.json). Each has 8 tiers (tier 1 is getting it; tiers 2 to 8 are 7 upgrades). Units and seconds as in the file. The garage damage bonus multiplies all of them.

- **Neon underglow:** circle of radius 2.5 around the car; 2 damage (half a minion's health, T-409) when an enemy enters and every 1 second inside, and each hit pushes a minion 0.5 units away from the car (the kaiju 0.1). ### Bricks

What the built weapon does in the game (built in task T-324). It follows Decisions ("Bricks", 2026-10-28), takes every number from [../game/data/weapons.json](../game/data/weapons.json) (the numbers below) and shows the card texts below. How the code is put together: `game/docs/weapons/bricks.md`, "Bricks".

- **Owning it:** a run starts without it. It is one of the car's weapons, offered on level-up cards like the other five, gained and upgraded through the same call (`upgrade("bricks")`); since T-321 it can be on the very first gift pack. A playtest switch (`game/data/playtest.json`, `bricks_at_start`, off) gives it at the start of every run.
- **The landing:** each step the weapon reads whether the car is in the air (the car's height model, the same airborne state the airtime trick uses) and counts the seconds; the step the car is back on the ground is the landing. A landing after at least the minimum time in the air (0.3 seconds) sends one impact wave; a shorter hop (a curb, a bump, a low step down) sends none. Any way of being in the air counts: a ramp's top, a rooftop's edge, any drop. Nothing is counted while the run is paused or after it ends.
- **The wave:** one wave at the landing spot, the same whatever the height of the fall (the impact speed is not read). Every enemy whose body is within the radius (4 units at tier 1) takes the damage once (6 at tier 1, times the garage damage bonus), the kaiju too: a minion counts when its body's edge is inside, an enemy that reports its body size (the kaiju) when any part of its body is. Nothing outside the radius is touched, and the steps after the landing send nothing more. Only the enemies on the level where the car lands are hit and pushed (since T-326, "Weapons hit only their own level" below). Minions that survive are pushed straight away from the landing spot by the knockback (4 units at tier 1), through the same call as the wiper's push; the kaiju is never pushed; a minion the wave kills is not pushed.
- **Tier 8, Widest Wave** (built in T-498, Decisions, "The board's own upgrade trees"): the radius is 3 times tier 7's (25.2 units), and the wave is 3 crash rings, at the tier 7 radius (8.4), twice it (16.8) and three times it (25.2). The inner ring is the usual wave and hits on the landing; then the wave travels outward: each next ring sets off from the one inside it when that one reaches its radius and races out to its own, so the middle ring reaches 16.8 half a second after the landing and the outer ring reaches 25.2 0.15 seconds later. Each ring hits, as the wave reaches it, the enemies then within its radius that no ring of this landing has hit yet: every enemy in reach is hit exactly once per landing, by the first ring that reaches it, with the tier's damage; minions it does not kill are pushed straight away from the landing spot (a minion pushed out of the inner ring is not hit again by the middle one); the kaiju is damaged, never pushed. The wave shows for 0.9 seconds. The timing (0.15 seconds a ring, `ring_interval` in the data) is T-498's proposal for the playtest.
- **Two landings close together** (the cooldown, 0.5 seconds): a landing less than the cooldown after the last wave sends none, so a bounce cannot stack two waves on the same enemies.
- **Tiers:** as the numbers below (radius, damage and knockback multipliers), applied from code; the drawn ring follows the radius.
- **The car:** its handling is not changed; "making the car heavier" is only the card's joke.
- **Look:** T-325's art (Visual style, Decisions, "Bricks' art"): at the landing spot (on a rooftop, on the roof) an impact ring on the ground, the circle as seen in the isometric view, growing in stepped frames from the car's size out to exactly the damage radius in about a third of a second and showing for 0.6 seconds, with dust clouds and brick chips thrown up, then crumbling away; one script-drawn sheet per tier radius. At tier 8 the sheet (T-498, drawn by the same script, no PixelLab) is the three rings: the tier 7 wave as it is, then a ring leaving it for 16.8 and another leaving that one for 25.2, each with its own dust and brick chips (more of them round the longer rings) and each crumbling once it is out, so for a moment all three show at once. The level-up card (and the HUD icon) shows T-325's PixelLab picture of a stack of bricks. T-324's code-drawn placeholder stays as the fallback for a radius with no sheet. No sound yet (the SFX bus has no fitting sound; a separate task can add one).

**Numbers** (proposed in task T-323): proposals by the Game Data Agent for the board's playtest, for the board's decision in Decisions ("Bricks", 2026-10-28). Numbers: `bricks` in [../game/data/weapons.json](../game/data/weapons.json), read through `WeaponData.bricks_at(tier)`. The weapon itself is T-324, which turned it on at level-ups (`offered_in_level_ups` true, `bricks` in `WEAPON_ORDER` and the choice ids in `level_up_offers.gd`). Same 8 tiers as the other weapons (tier 1 is getting it, tiers 2 to 8 are 7 upgrades; the card texts, T-322, are written for that count). The garage damage bonus multiplies its damage.

- **Damage 6**, once per enemy per landing. A minion has 4 health, so the first tier already kills a minion outright, as the board's "powerful impact wave" asks. A landing is rare (see below), so one hit has to be worth far more than the wiper's 3 per 0.5 seconds.
- **Radius 4** units around the car, a disc about 50 square units against the wiper's half disc of radius 2.5 (about 10). It is wide because the wave is the point of the weapon, and a player lands rarely.
- **Knockback 4** units, minions only, never the kaiju (the wiper's rule). Smaller than the wiper's 6: the wave is meant to clear space around the car and kill, and a minion that survives (kaiju excluded) is not thrown out of reach.
- **Minimum time in the air 0.3 seconds**, the same as the airtime trick's `min_seconds` (`combo.json`, Drifting, Content, "Airtime trick"): a curb or a bump stays under it, a ramp hop (about 0.7 seconds) and a rooftop fall (about 1 second) are well over it. One number for "a real jump" keeps the two features agreeing.
- **Cooldown 0.5 seconds** between waves, so a bounce or two quick landings cannot stack waves on the same enemies. It is rarely felt: a jump takes over 0.3 seconds in the air plus time on the ground.
- **Same wave whatever the height** (first build); the height does not scale any number.
- **Tiers** (the board's own tree, T-496, replacing T-323's): 2 radius x1.4; 3 damage x1.5; 4 knockback x1.5; 5 radius x1.5; 6 damage x1.5; 7 knockback x1.5 (at tier 7: radius 8.4, damage 13.5, knockback 9). 8 Widest Wave (T-498): radius x3, the wave in 3 rings (effect `widest_wave`, value 3: the radius times 3 and that many rings; `ring_interval` 0.15 seconds between the rings reaching their radii, a proposal), so at tier 8: radius 25.2, damage 13.5, knockback 9. The wave art is one script-drawn sheet per radius (4, 5.6, 8.4 and, for tier 8, 25.2).
- **How it compares:** the wiper at tier 1 does about 6 damage per second to an enemy it keeps hitting; the underglow 2 per second. Landings on the elevation map come from ramps and rooftop edges only, perhaps one every 10 to 20 seconds of a run that uses them, so Bricks' average damage per second to one enemy is about 0.3 to 0.6 at tier 1, but it hits everything within 4 units at once and kills a minion, so against a crowd it matches the wiper and fires only when the player jumps. If the board finds it too weak or too strong in playtest, damage and radius are the first numbers to move.

### The windshield wiper (built in task T-170)

What the built wiper does in the game. It follows Decisions ("Three new weapons" and the board's answers: it sweeps all the time, damages the kaiju, pushes only minions) and takes every number from [../game/data/weapons.json](../game/data/weapons.json) (the proposal above). How the code is put together: `game/docs/weapons/windshield-wiper.md`, "The windshield wiper".

It is one of the car's weapons, gained and upgraded through the same call as the others (`upgrade("wiper")`), offered at a level-up (the level-up offers, below). A playtest switch (`game/data/playtest.json`, `wiper_at_start`, off) gives it at the start of every run.
- **The arm** (the Weapon Behavior Agent's proposal, for the board's playtest): one arm turning round a point on the nose of the car's body (half a unit ahead of its centre), 2.5 units long at tier 1. It sweeps from one side of the car to the other and back across the semicircle in front of the car (180 degrees) at 240 degrees per second, all the time, turning back at each end. The semicircle is fixed to the car, so it turns when the car turns.
- **Hits:** an enemy is hit when the arm touches its body as it sweeps. Each hit is the tier's damage times the garage damage bonus, through the enemy's damage function. **How often** (proposal): one enemy is hit at most once per pass (one sweep from one side to the other), and never twice within 0.5 seconds (the data's hit cooldown). So an enemy that stays in front of the car is hit about once per pass, about every 0.75 seconds at tier 1.
- **Knockback:** a hit minion is pushed 6 units (tier 1; 9 at the last tier) straight away from the car's centre, through the minion's knockback call (obstacles stop it). The kaiju is damaged but never pushed. The gun's bullets and the flamethrower still never push (tested).

- **Tiers:** as the proposal above (length, sweep speed and knockback multipliers), applied from code. **Tier 8, the Back Wiper (T-499):** weapons.json's effect `add_back_wiper` makes a second wiper (the same script, flagged `is_back`) that the front one creates and steps. It has the same tier, so the same stats; its pivot is half a unit behind the car's centre (the tail) and its arm is the mirror image of the front's across the car's cross axis (it sweeps the semicircle behind the car, starting on the same side as the front arm and turning the other way). It keeps its own hit record, so its hit cooldown and once-per-pass rule are its own; its hits come out of the front wiper's `enemy_hit` signal. It is drawn with the same blade art, picked by the arm's direction. Below tier 8, or in a new run, it does not exist.
- **Look:** a placeholder wiper drawn in code: a dark steel arm with a pale grey rubber blade and a dark outline, from the car's nose out to the arm's tip, drawn over the car while it points down the screen and under it while it points up. Never in Greenbull's colours. The final art replaces it.

### Weapons hit only their own level (built in task T-326)

What the game does on a map with rooftops and ramps (the elevation map, and levels from the level creator). It follows Decisions ("Weapons hit only enemies on the car's own level", 2026-10-28). Points marked "proposal" go beyond the decision and wait for the board's playtest. How it is built: `game/docs/weapons/shared.md`, "Weapons", "Levels".

- **Whose level:** the car's level for the starting gun, the exhaust flamethrower, the neon underglow, the windshield wiper (its push too) and Bricks (its damage and its push; the wave is on the level of the ground where the car lands). A molotov's fire burns on its own level, the ground's height where the bottle landed, wherever the car is by then. An enemy on another level inside a weapon's reach is not damaged and not pushed.
- **The starting gun:** it aims only at enemies on the car's level, so it does not fire (and wastes no volley) when the only enemies in range are on another level. Each bullet hits only enemies on the level it was fired on and flies past the rest.
- **The car's level:** the height of the ground under the car's centre: the street, the rooftop or the point of the ramp it stands on.
- **In the air (proposal):** the level under the car, the ground right below its centre. A car jumping across a rooftop still hits the enemies on that rooftop; once it is over the street, the ones on the street. Chosen over "nothing until it lands" so the weapons do not stop at every jump, and over the car's own height in the air so a high jump does not switch every weapon off.
- **A ramp's in-between heights (proposal): a tolerance of 1.5 units.** An enemy counts as on the level when its height is within 1.5 units of it. On a ramp (3 units of rise over 12) that is 6 units along the slope either way, so an enemy part way up a ramp next to the car is hit, while a rooftop is always at least 3 units from the street and the next rooftop, so nothing on another rooftop or the street below ever is. A falling enemy counts at its height in the air. The enemies' contact damage uses 1 unit (Enemies, Content, "Contact damage at the car's level"); the weapons' wider 1.5 lets a ramp's slope count a little more generously.
- **The kaiju (proposal): yes, on every level.** It is taller than the highest rooftop, so every weapon hits it from the street, a rooftop or a ramp, as before. Its push rules are unchanged (the wiper and Bricks never push it).
- **The MVP map:** a map without heights has no levels, so every weapon hits every enemy in reach exactly as before. An enemy that reports no height of its own also counts on every level.

### Size upgrades grow on screen (built in task T-286)

What the built grow does. It follows Decisions ("Size upgrades grow on screen"). The numbers and reasons are the Weapon Behavior Agent's proposal for the board's playtest; the code is in `game/docs/weapons/shared.md`, "Weapons", "Size upgrades grow".

- **Which tiers grow:** the ones whose effect in `weapons.json` is a size. The windshield wiper's tiers 2, 5 and 7 (arm length x1.2, x1.3, x1.3), the neon underglow's tiers 2, 5 and 8 (radius x1.2, x1.25, x1.3) and the exhaust flamethrower's tiers 2, 4 and 6 (flame size x1.2, x1.3, x1.3). Every other tier, and getting a weapon, plays no grow. The molotovs' tiers 2 and 7 (fire radius) play none either: each fire area is made when a bottle lands and keeps its size, so the next bottle just makes a bigger one and there is no standing weapon to grow (the board can ask for the fires on the ground to grow too).
- **When:** when the card or gift pack that raises the size is picked. The run is paused for the pick, so the grow starts as the game resumes and stands still while it is paused.
- **The curve (one place, `game/scripts/weapons/size_grow.gd`):** the size goes from the old size to the new one in **0.4 seconds** with a small overshoot (an ease-out-back, strength 3.0, about 20% of the step: a step is 20 to 30% of the size, so the size passes the new one by about 5% and settles) and ends exactly on the data's size. 0.4 seconds is long enough to read as growing and short enough that nobody waits for it; the overshoot is a small pop and not a wobble. The likes bar's spring (0.25 s, T-273) is the model; a weapon is bigger and further from the eye, so it gets a little longer.
- **Damage follows the drawing:** the wiper's reach, the underglow's circle and the flame's outline are all read from the size being drawn at each step, so nothing is hit outside what is drawn (and the overshoot is hit as drawn). Damage, timing and everything else are unchanged.
- **The wiper's art now follows its length (T-286):** the wiper's sheet was drawn for the first tier's 2.5 units and was never stretched, so the drawn blade stayed the same size at every tier while only the hit area grew. The art is now stretched by the arm's length over 2.5, so the drawing grows with the tiers and the hit area is the drawing. The underglow's ring and the flame already scaled with their size.

### The level-up offers (built in tasks T-043 and T-172)

What the built level-up offers do. It follows Decisions ("Level-up flow in the MVP" and "Level-up offers with five weapons"). The screen that shows them is a separate UI task; how the code is put together: `game/docs/weapons/shared.md`, "Weapons", "The level-up offers".

- When a level-up's weapon choice starts (the run is paused for it, Game loop architecture), the car's weapons decide the choices. Each of the weapons (six since T-324 added Bricks) gives at most one available choice: "get" it while the car does not have it, or "upgrade" it (its next tier) once it does, until its last tier (8 for every weapon, each weapon's own last tier in the data; Bricks' 8 came with T-498 and the wiper's with T-499). The gun is always owned, so it only has "upgrade the gun". That makes 11 possible choices: upgrade the gun, and get or upgrade the flamethrower, the neon underglow, the molotovs, the windshield wiper and Bricks.
- Each level-up offers 2 different choices drawn at random, all equally likely, from every choice available at that moment. They are shown in a fixed order (gun, flamethrower, underglow, molotovs, wiper, Bricks), not in the order drawn.
- The first level-up is drawn like every other one (Decisions, "The first gift pack can hold any weapon", T-321): two different choices at random from every choice available, so it can offer any weapon the car doesn't have (five besides the gun, with Bricks), or the gun's upgrade. A run that already starts with the flamethrower (its playtest switch) draws the same way, and the flamethrower's upgrades are among the choices.
- Taking a choice applies it at once (the weapon at tier 1, or its next tier) and the run resumes. If several levels were reached at once, their choices follow one after another, each drawn on its own.
- A weapon at its last tier is not offered. If only one choice is left, the level-up offers just that one (the docs say nothing here; the simplest case was taken). If none is left, the choice is skipped and the run goes on, as Decisions says.
- **Playtest switch** `all_weapons_at_start` (`game/data/playtest.json`, false, never visible to players): true gives the car every weapon (all six) at tier 1 at the start of every run, for the integration check and the board's playtest; the level-ups then offer only upgrades.
