# HUD and menus

## Summary

During a run the HUD shows the car's HP, a timer counting down from 8:00, the XP bar and level, the coins collected, and the kaiju's health once it appears. Levelling up pauses the game and shows the weapon choice as simple cards. Escape pauses the game, and the pause screen has a camera zoom slider. In the MVP every screen is kept plain; their visual style comes after the MVP.

## Decisions

## Content

### Main menu

A plain screen (task T-054) with a dark navy background. The title "SOLID CARBIDE" is in big cyan letters, with a small grey "(work in progress)" under it. Below are three buttons in a column: Start, Garage and Exit (text approved in T-053). The menu only shows the screen: pressing a button sends the signal `start_pressed`, `garage_pressed` or `exit_pressed`, and what happens next is the screen flow (T-055). Keyboard focus starts on Start. The scene is `game/scenes/menu/main_menu.tscn`; how to open it is in `game/docs/hud-and-menus/main-menu.md`, "Main menu".

### The HUD

The first piece of the HUD (task T-090) is the run timer. It shows the time left in minutes and seconds (M:SS), counting down from 8:00 to 0:00, since T-335 as a digital clock in a dark panel at the bottom left of the screen (see "Timer clock" below); until then it was big cyan digits at the top centre. It only shows what the run reports (the run owns the time) and stops moving while the game is paused. It needs no text. It is shown in every run, whether the run scene is opened on its own or started from the main menu. The rest of the HUD (task T-049, a first layout the board adjusts in playtest) is in the same scene, `game/scenes/hud/hud.tscn`, which the run scene places on screen; how to open it is in `game/docs/game-loop/the-run.md`, "The run". ### Trick popups

Task T-091. The thread the needle trick (T-271; Level design, Decisions, "Thread the needle"), driving between two obstacles the board marked as a pair, pops **THREAD THE NEEDLE!** followed by "+{amount}", for T-272 to use word for word: the board's name for the trick, in capitals with an exclamation mark like the others. Extended-drift popups are smaller, since they come every second. The look (task T-084, reworked after the board's playtest of 2026-10-13) is a comic-book popup: a white rounded rectangle (stepped pixel corners, not a jagged burst, board 2026-10-14) fitted to the text with a small padding, with a black border, every letter black (trick names and amounts alike) in the Bangers comic font on one straight baseline, with hard pixel edges (no smoothing) and no other colour. Black on white reads over both the dark asphalt and the dusty gravel. One popup pixel is two art pixels. Bangers has capitals only, so "Trending! +{likes} Likes" shows in capitals. It sits near the car, clear of the run timer at the top centre. Since task T-125 it follows the car for its whole life (Decisions, 2026-10-13): it is placed relative to the car's drawn position (the position the camera follows, set in the physics step so it never shakes), still pops above the car, grows, rises relative to the car and fades. Popups shown close together (a long drift every second plus a 360, or several tricks on one step) are stacked: a new popup that would overlap one already showing is moved up in steps of 4 pixels until it is clear, with a 4 pixel gap, and it stays at that place relative to the car. Since the board's playtest of 2026-10-14 popups are smaller and never go higher than half way from the car to the top of the screen: the highest popup's top edge stays at or below a quarter of the screen height from the top, at any window size and camera zoom (measured on screen). At most three popups show at once (a fourth makes the oldest go away), and they are small enough that three stack under that limit at the default zoom (14.4) and window (1152x648): the popup starts about 30 pixels above the car and rises about 20, and it rises fast at first and slows towards the end, where it fades (a cubic ease-out over the same one second). The challenge popup and the drift prototype follow the car the same way. It works in the run, including runs started from the main menu, and in the drift prototype. The challenge popup (task T-092) reuses it: when a challenge is completed, the run state's `score` signal shows "Trending! +{likes} Likes" (text approved in T-048, `hud.score_flash`) from the car in the same look and size as the normal trick popups, with the likes the challenge gave. Since task T-083 each trick popup also shows what the trick added to the combo, after its name, in the approved "+{amount}" text (T-082): "LONG DRIFT! +0.05", "FULL 360! +0.25", "ALT DRIFT! +0.5". At the combo cap the trick adds nothing and only the name shows. In the run the popups follow the run state's combo signal; the drift prototype has no combo and shows the name only. How it is built is in `game/docs/hud-and-menus/popups.md`, "Trick popups". The popups are always drawn above every building, sign, boulder and the car (task T-132, board 2026-10-14): a popup has its own absolute depth at the top of the game's drawing range, not tied to the map's depth numbers, so a skyscraper or a short building never hides one, however tall the map grows. They still draw under the HUD and the pause and level-up screens. The popups heat up with the combo (task T-133, Decisions, board 2026-10-14), on the same steps as the HUD's combo counter (same thresholds, one place): a trick popup takes the step of the multiplier the trick brought the combo to, and the challenge popup the combo's multiplier when it shows. Below x2 the popup is the plain black and white one above, with no colour. From x2 (step 1) the black border becomes a thin black outline round a yellow ring (the counter is yellow at this step). From x3 (step 2) the ring is wider, orange with a yellow inner ring, and the "+amount" is orange with a thin black outline. From x4 up (step 3) the popup also has a flaming edge: pixel flames in the burning likes bar's style and colours (white-hot next to the popup, then yellow, orange-yellow and orange at the tips, the odd spark), tall over the top edge, short at the sides and below, flickering in pixel-art frames (twelve a second). The hotter rings and flames reach a little past the plain popup, but stacking, the height limit and the three-popup limit use the plain popup's size, so they work as before. The drift prototype has no combo and keeps the plain popup.

### Pause screen

Tasks T-015, T-093, T-095 and T-124. Escape pauses the whole game and shows the pause screen; Escape again resumes from exactly where the car was. The screen is a compact, nearly opaque black strip across the whole width, starting just below the HUD timer and only as tall as its content, over a light dim of the rest of the screen, so as much of the map as possible stays visible. It works in the run (including runs started from the main menu) and in the drift prototype. From top to bottom, centred in the strip:

- **Title:** "Paused", in large white letters.
- **Hint:** "Press Escape to get back to the stream." (texts from T-047).
- **Zoom slider:** "Camera zoom level" (T-093), a slider from 6 to 20 (default 14.4) with its value as a number at the right end. Moving it zooms the view behind the strip at once while the game stays paused, and the value is saved.
- **SFX volume slider** (T-077): "SFX volume" (the board's name), a slider from 0 to 100 with its value as a number at the right end, on its own row directly under the zoom slider, laid out exactly like it (label, slider of the same width, value at the right end). It starts at 0, which is silent, so sound effects are silent until the player turns it up. Moving it changes the volume of every sound effect at once (the sounds' own volumes are in `game/data/sfx.json`; at 100 they play at those volumes, at 0 nothing is heard), and the value is saved between sessions like the zoom.
- **Music volume slider** (T-208): "Music volume", one short plain settings line at the left of the slider, the sibling of "SFX volume" and "Camera zoom level". T-210 uses this text word for word. The slider sits on its own row directly under the SFX volume slider, laid out like it. Built in T-210: it starts from the saved music setting (T-209, starting value 40, a proposal for the playtest), changes the music at once while the music keeps playing under the pause, and is saved between sessions. The strip is one row taller (it ends about 290 pixels from the top at 1152x648, about 325 with the dynamic-camera buttons), still below the HUD timer.
- **Camera checkboxes** (T-124): "Camera smoothing" and "Dynamic camera" side by side, the board's own names (what they do: Drifting, Content, "Camera options"). Each starts from the saved setting, applies at once when switched and is saved. Placeholder look: a light square frame on a dark fill, with a light square inside when checked, so both states show over any ground.
- **Tuning buttons** (T-124), shown only while "Dynamic camera" is on: "Set min zoom" and "Set max zoom", each followed by the stored value as a number (for example 11.0 and 17.0, the proposed starting values). Pressing one stores the zoom slider's current value as the dynamic camera's zoom at a standstill (min) or at top forward speed (max), saves it, and updates the number. The board can move the slider, press a button and resume to try it. They are a temporary tuning tool for the board, plain labels rather than game text, and are removed once the board has settled the values. Placeholder look: a light frame round a dark fill.

The checkboxes and buttons work with the mouse and the keyboard (Tab to move, Space to switch or press). The strip's dark band runs over the lower part of the car's health bar while paused. How it is built is in `game/docs/hud-and-menus/pause-screen.md`, "Pause screen".

### Recap screen
