# HUD and menus

## Summary

During a run the HUD shows the car's HP, a timer counting down from 8:00, the XP bar and level, the coins collected, and the kaiju's health once it appears. Levelling up pauses the game and shows the weapon choice as simple cards. Escape pauses the game, and the pause screen has a camera zoom slider. In the MVP every screen is kept plain; their visual style comes after the MVP.

## Content

### The HUD's style in code (T-429, goal G-008; proposals for the playtest)

The HUD pieces follow the HUD style sheet (Visual style, Decisions, "The HUD style sheet"); the values sit in `hud_style.gd` (`game/docs/hud-and-menus/hud.md`). Words are Bangers with the near-black outline (8, 8, 15): outline 6 on the coins, the sub goal, "WATCHING" and the flight and drop numbers; 8 on the big numbers (weapon counts, airtime and drift counters, the viewers number, the combo heat steps). The trick and combo broken popups use that near-black for letters and borders, the kaiju bar for its outline and dividers (2 px). The pause strip's title and text are Bangers, shown in capitals (the text itself is unchanged; its three slider numbers stay in the default font, since Bangers would make the strip 10 pixels shorter); the level-up title has outline 6. The **clock** is T-339's art: the steel panel at 2x (exactly its 200 x 72) and the drawn seven-segment digits at 2x, tinted with the time step's colour over a dim unlit 8 (the step colour at 22%, a proposal); no system font is left in the HUD. The **weapon icons** are their own 24 x 24 art (T-429, script-drawn from the card pictures, 0 PixelLab; it scored 7 of 10 in the style loop, kept for the playtest) shown at 2x in the same 48 pixel slot, so every HUD sprite now has the same pixel size.

### The shared button (T-446; proposals for the playtest)

Every button the player meets is one shared button (Decisions, "One button style across the game"): the main menu's three, the garage's Buy and Back, the tutorial slides' two arrows and Start (and the "Don't show again" checkbox, styled to match), the pause screen's Trick list, Restart, Exit and the trick list's Back (and its two camera checkboxes), and the recap's Continue and X. It is defined once, beside the HUD style sheet's values in `game/scripts/hud/hud_style.gd` (`HudStyle.style_button`, `HudStyle.style_checkbox`); the screens only call it and keep their buttons' text, place, size, keys and actions. The board's tools keep their own look: the level creator, the drift tuning panel and the pause strip's two camera tuning buttons (Set min and max zoom).

- **Plate** (T-447): one 9-slice plate per state in `game/art/ui/` (`button_plate_normal.png`, `_hover`, `_focus`, `_pressed`, `_disabled`; record `button_plates.json`), 72 x 22 art pixels shown at 2x, slice margins 3 art pixels; the plates are drawn in the HUD style sheet's steel (Visual style, Content, "The shared button's plates"). No tint and no recolour in code: every state is its own picture. The level-up screen's own plate (`game/art/level_up/button_plate*.png`) stays as that screen's art but is no longer used by the button.
- **Text:** Bangers in capitals, the sheet's light label colour (235, 242, 255) with its near-black outline 6; size 28, and 16 with outline 4 on the pause strip's small buttons.
- **States and feel:** hovered shows the brighter hover plate and turns the text white; keyboard-selected draws the focus plate, a bright ring with an open middle, over whichever state the button is in, and the text turns white; pressed shows the sunk plate with the text 2 pixels lower (the press-down); disabled shows the dim plate and greys the text (the garage's Buy with too few coins). The mouse pointer becomes a hand over a button. The tutorial arrows are drawn in the text colour with the outline and drop with the press too; the recap's X is white with the outline.
- **Checkbox:** no plate; the same text, the same keyboard ring plate, and a square with a light frame on steel, a light mark when checked.

### The HUD scales with the window (T-431, goal G-008; proposals for the playtest)

Decisions, 'The HUD scales with the window, and a HUD size slider'. **The scaling rule:** the HUD is laid out on the board's layout page (1152 x 648), and at any window size the whole HUD is drawn at one scale: the smaller of the window's width over 1152 and its height over 648, times the HUD size slider's value in percent. Each piece keeps its corner or edge (hearts and weapon icons top left, coins and clock bottom left, viewers counter bottom right, the sub goal on the right edge at its share of the height) and its margins and size grow and shrink with that scale, so the HUD covers the same share of the screen at any resolution. On a wider screen than 16:9 (the 3440 x 1440 ultrawide) the height decides, and the pieces stay at the far corners; on a narrower one the width decides. Examples at 100: 1 at 1152 x 648, 1.67 at 1920 x 1080, 2.22 at 2560 x 1440 and 3440 x 1440, 0.83 at 960 x 540. The scale is often not a whole number, so the HUD's art is drawn with hard pixel edges (nearest filtering) instead; some art pixels are then one screen pixel wider than others, which the playtest judges. The world camera's zoom is never changed by the HUD. When the HUD size is above 100 the sub goal square moves up just enough to stay 16 layout pixels above the live viewers counter, so they never overlap.

**HUD size slider:** on the pause screen (below, "Pause screen"), from **50 to 125**, in steps of 5, **default 100** (the size the board laid the HUD out at, in proportion to the window). Above 125, all five weapons' icon column would reach the coins counter, since the layout page is already full at 100. Moving it resizes the HUD at once behind the pause strip; it is saved on this computer when the drag ends (and when the pause screen closes) and used at the next start. **Which screens it covers:** the run's HUD only (hearts, weapon icons, coins, clock, sub goal, live viewers counter, reaction icons, the airtime and drift counters and the viewers flying into the counter). The pause screen itself, the level-up, time's up and recap screens, the menus and garage, and the trick popups and the kaiju's health bar (drawn in the world, at the camera's zoom) keep their size. How it is built: `game/docs/hud-and-menus/hud.md`, "The HUD scales with the window", and `pause-screen.md`.

### The HUD layout editor (T-453; proposals for the playtest)

**The slides screen (task T-382).** Three full-screen slides on the main menu's dark background, a pale pink arrow triangle on each side (hidden where there is no slide to go to) and three squares at the bottom showing which slide it is. Slide 1: the two controls lines, centred. Slide 2: the seven tricks in a grid of four columns, each a still illustration (64 x 48 pixels drawn at 3 times, nearest) with its name below, then the two loop lines; until the art tickets' PNGs are in, each shows a code-made placeholder (blue picture, pink arrow). Slide 3: the goal line, the "Don't show again" checkbox and the Start button. Pressing Start sends the checkbox state to the screen flow, which starts the run and, when it is ticked, saves `tutorial_hidden` in the settings. Keys: left/right arrow or A/D move between slides, Enter presses the focused control (proposal). The trick list is its own scene so the pause screen can reuse it. How it is built: `game/docs/hud-and-menus/tutorial-slides.md`.

Proposals for the board to judge: the Start button reads "Go live" (loop score 9) where the board's word is "Start"; swap it in `button.start` if "Start" is wanted, like the main menu. Slide 2's single line failed the loop (card_desc is 90 characters at most; best 7), so it is two lines.

### Tutorial clips (T-483; recordings for the redrawn slides)

Decisions, "The tutorial slides are redrawn, with clips of the real car". Four looping GIFs of the real car (its code and art, driven by scripted inputs) on the drift prototype's plain grid, 480 x 320 pixels, one shared palette, 25 frames a second for the drift clip (camera held still) and 16.7 for the other three (every third physics step, to stay under 1.5 MB), nothing else on screen (no HUD, popups, enemies, city or test room): `game/art/tutorial/clips/drift.gif` (slide 1: zoomed out, the camera held still, the car driving straight, breaking into a drift with W + A, its tyre marks tracing the path from where the drift starts), `long_drift.gif`, `alt_drift.gif` (a forward drift W + A, then a backward one S + A, which counts as ALT DRIFT!) and `ramp_jump.gif` (slide 2: the elevation test scene's ramp, hang time and an air spin in one clip). Each has a record JSON beside it (`<name>.json`: size, frames, seconds, bytes, the tricks and the airtime the run printed). Nothing in the game uses them yet; the UI task puts them on the slides.

**Re-recording:** from the repo root, `python game/tools/record_tutorial_clips.py` (needs a real window, not headless; Godot's movie maker, about 15 seconds a clip; `--only NAME ...` records some, `--no-archive` skips copying the take to `art-archive/T-483/takes/`). The fixture scenes are `game/tests/fixtures/tutorial_clip_<name>.tscn` (script `tutorial_clip_demo.gd`: the inputs, the zoom, the camera and the start are exported values in each scene).
